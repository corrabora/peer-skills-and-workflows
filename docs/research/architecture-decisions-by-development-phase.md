# Commitment, not calendar, sets architecture deadlines

No canon requires "the architecture" before a phase; each requires a small class of decisions, plus evidence that they are feasible, before the next escalation of commitment: money, headcount, a supplier contract, a certification plan or a second team. Two architecture gates recur under different names: a feasibility gate (at least one workable architecture, with the infeasible options named: Boehm's LCO, NASA's MCR, DoD's ASR, ECSS's PRR) and a commitment gate (architecture chosen, requirements and criticality allocated to elements, interfaces baselined, major risks retired: Boehm's LCA, and PDR ahead of NASA's KDP C or DoD's Milestone B), followed by build-to and operational-readiness gates. Every school, including the deferral advocates, places the same decisions early: the quality attributes and criticality levels to protect, the technology stack and top-level style, the decomposition that teams, suppliers and independence arguments will follow, data ownership and the tenant or partition key, external interfaces and trust boundaries, and anything with a long lead time. Regulated domains turn this into a dependency chain (hazard or threat analysis, then requirements carrying criticality, then an architecture that allocates it, then verification) that constrains the evidence for each released baseline, not the calendar. The evidence is convergent in direction and weak in precision: Boehm's COCOMO II analysis of **161 projects** is the only quantified rule for sizing architecture effort, GAO's portfolio data and the Conway's-law studies are the best field data, and the numbers in general circulation (100x cost escalation, the "IBM Systems Sciences Institute" ratios, Standish failure rates) are context-bound or untraceable. **No study found varies the timing of architecture decisions while holding effort constant**, so the central claim of every canon is an argued position that size and criticality make more plausible and volatility less. For a founder the working rule is to date the few irreversible and long-lead decisions explicitly, buy reversibility for the rest, and treat an increment, not the system, as the unit of commitment.

## Two architecture gates recur in every canon, under different names

The canons use different lifecycles, yet their gates line up once each is read as a commitment point and not a calendar phase. The alignment below is a synthesis across the sources; no single source states it. The mapping of ICSM reviews to DoD milestones is Boehm's own and not DoD policy ([Boehm and Lane 2009](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2009/systemengr/9021WednesdayTrack7Boehm.pdf)), and ECSS's PRR both closes feasibility and selects the concept, so it straddles two rows. Throughout, **N** marks a normative requirement ("shall", "will" or statute) and **G** marks handbook, guidebook or method guidance. Clause-level content of paywalled standards comes from catalogue pages, regulator documents and named secondary sources, because the standard texts themselves were not read.

Four patterns hold across the rows. First, the binding external commitment always follows the architecture baseline and the preliminary design: NASA's Agency Baseline Commitment is set at KDP C ([NPR 7120.5F Ch. 2](https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_7120_005F_&page_name=Chapter2)), DoD's Acquisition Program Baseline at Milestone B after a statutory PDR ([10 U.S.C. 4252](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/4252)), and Boehm writes that for large systems passing LCA "is the point of significant escalation of staff level and resource commitments" ([Boehm 2000](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA382590.pdf)). No framework permits the external commitment at the requirements review. Second, interfaces are frozen earlier than the things they connect: external interfaces at SRR, internal interfaces in preliminary form at the architecture baseline, interface control documents at PDR, and DoD's modular system interfaces, presented with the programme's open-systems approach at Milestone B ([NPR 7123.1D App. G](https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_7123_001D_&page_name=AppendixG); [DoDI 5000.88](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/500088p.pdf)). Third, the size of the gate package is risk-driven and never fixed numerically: Boehm asks for a "risk-driven level of detail for each element", NASA expects its review criteria to be tailored, and NASA's own MDR/SDR success criterion accepts that some trades remain open provided those "planned for Phase B adequately address the option space" ([Boehm 2000](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA382590.pdf); [NPR 7123.1D App. G](https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_7123_001D_&page_name=AppendixG)). Fourth, in iterative lifecycles the gates do not disappear; they move inside the loop and attach to an increment. GAO's account of leading companies has teams "stop designing hardware for the given MVP" at the commitment point while software continues to change ([GAO-23-106222](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106222)), and DO-178C orders work by applicant-defined transition criteria and traceability, not by time ([AdaCore summary of DO-178C objectives](https://learn-latest.adacore.com/booklets/adacore-technologies-for-airborne-software/chapters/summary.html)).

## Systems engineering baselines the architecture one review before the money

The generic standards deliberately decline to say when. ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288:2023 (Edition 2, May 2023) defines technical processes by outcome and does not prescribe a life cycle model ([ISO 15288:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/81702.html)); SEBoK's reading is that process execution "is not compartmentalized to particular life cycle stages" ([SEBoK, Generic Life Cycle Model](https://sebokwiki.org/wiki/Generic_Life_Cycle_Model)). The 2015 edition split the old Architectural Design process into Architecture Definition (the complete solution and all stakeholders' concerns) and Design Definition (each system of interest) so that the architecture stays "design-agnostic to maximum extent possible" ([Martin 2015](https://www.incose.org/wp-content/uploads/legacy/presentation/IS2015-pdf/5-2-2.pdf)). That split is the standards-level basis for baselining architecture ahead of design. The 2023 edition refined the system architecture definition process and added an informative MBSE annex ([INCITS](https://www.incits.org/news-events/news-coverage/revision-of-cornerstone-life-cycle-management-standard-isoiecieee-15288); [ANSI](https://blog.ansi.org/ansi/iso-iec-ieee-15288-2023-system-life-cycle-processes/)). ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010:2022 specifies what an architecture description must contain and explicitly excludes the processes and methods by which one is produced ([ISO 42010:2022](https://www.iso.org/standard/74393.html)). ISO/IEC/IEEE 42020:2019 says its processes apply "within any stage in the life cycle", and both 42020 and the evaluation framework 42030 were **placed under revision on 8 January 2026** ([ISO 42020:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/68982.html); [ISO 42030:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/73436.html)). The nearest thing to a gate in the generic guidance is SEBoK's entry condition for realization: the architecture and feasibility evidence must be "sufficiently low-risk" ([SEBoK, Generic Life Cycle Model](https://sebokwiki.org/wiki/Generic_Life_Cycle_Model)). The INCOSE handbook (5th ed., 2023) follows 15288 and leaves named gates to the domain ([O'Reilly listing](https://oreilly.com/library/view/incose-systems-engineering/9781119814290)). These statements rest on catalogue pages and secondary descriptions; none of the ISO texts or the handbook body was read.

NASA is the most explicit source in any canon. NPR 7123.1D (effective July 2023) makes a **"Baselined architecture definition"** [SE-41] and a baselined allocation of requirements to the next lower level [SE-42] minimum products at MDR/SDR, before KDP B and a full phase ahead of the "Preliminary design solution definition" required at PDR; CDR requires a "Baseline detailed design" ([NPR 7123.1D Ch. 5](https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_7123_001D_&page_name=Chapter5)). The technical baseline then advances from functional (SDR) to allocated (PDR), product (CDR) and as-deployed (ORR) ([NASA SE Handbook 6.5](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-5-configuration-management/)). The "shall" attaches to those minimum products. The detailed entrance and success criteria in Appendix G are "recommended best practices" that projects are expected to tailor, although a decision not to tailor "should be justified" ([NPR 7123.1D App. G](https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_7123_001D_&page_name=AppendixG)). Two details matter to a practitioner. The two NPRs differ by one review: NPR 7120.5F baselines the mission and spacecraft architecture at SRR and the ground and payload architectures at SDR/MDR, while 7123.1D places the baselined architecture definition at MDR/SDR; 7123.1D itself says 7120.5 takes precedence, and both land before KDP B ([NPR 7120.5F App. I](https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_7120_005F_&page_name=AppendixI); [NPR 7123.1D App. G](https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_7123_001D_&page_name=AppendixG)). And software architecture has no criterion of its own in the system-level review tables; it is delegated at every review to NASA-HDBK-2203, which was not opened ([NPR 7123.1D App. G](https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_7123_001D_&page_name=AppendixG)).

DoD names five technical baselines (mission, concept, functional, allocated, product) and ties three to reviews: functional at SFR, allocated at PDR, initial product at CDR ([DoDI 5000.88](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/500088p.pdf); [AAF SFR](https://aaf.waru.edu/mca/sfr/); [AAF CDR](https://aaf.waru.edu/mca/cdr/)). Guidance says the PDR "ensures the preliminary design and basic system architecture are complete" and that "computer system and software architecture designs have been established" ([AAF PDR](https://aaf.waru.edu/mca/pdr/)). Statute makes this the decisive gate: a major programme may not receive Milestone B approval until the decision authority confirms it has considered a set of factors, among them that the programme has received a PDR and that its technology "has been demonstrated in a relevant environment" ([10 U.S.C. 4252](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/4252)). A second statute requires a modular open systems approach "to the maximum extent practicable" ([10 U.S.C. 4401](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/4401)), policy requires the approach to be presented at Milestone B ([DoDI 5000.88](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/500088p.pdf)), and guidance pushes the supporting trade analysis back to "before contracts are let for technology development" ([DoD SE Guidebook](https://ac.cto.mil/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Systems-Eng-Guidebook_Feb2022-Cleared-slp.pdf)). MOSA is therefore a legally mandated instance of "fix the interfaces early, defer the modules". The Software Acquisition Pathway inverts the pattern. Entry to execution requires a capability needs statement, user agreement, acquisition strategy, test strategy and cost estimate, with **no architecture artifact** among the entry conditions (the execution-phase page does list an Information Support Plan that includes the system architecture among its regulatory documents); architecture language in planning is "should"; the binding commitments are a minimum viable capability release deployed "within 1 year", releases at least annually and active management of technical debt ([AAF, planning phase](https://aaf.waru.edu/aaf/software/planning-phase/); [AAF, execution phase](https://aaf.waru.edu/aaf/software/execution-phase/)).

A reader in late 2026 should treat the DoD material as a 2020 to 2022 policy set under rewrite. Section 4252 was restructured from certifications to "factors to be considered" in December 2024 and amended again in December 2025; JCIDS was replaced in January 2026 and DoDI 5000.02 took Change 2 in April 2026 ([10 U.S.C. 4252](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/4252); [AAF change log](https://aaf.waru.edu/aaf/policies/policies-change-log/)). A 7 November 2025 memorandum, read only through a vendor summary, announces fewer reviews and consolidated milestones ([Carahsoft summary](https://static.carahsoft.com/concrete/files/4917/6702/9385/Memorandum_Transforming_the_Defense_Acquisition_System_into_the_Warfighting_Acquisition_System.pdf)). The current texts of DoDI 5000.02 and 5000.87 could not be opened. IEEE 15288.1 and 15288.2 have been Inactive-Reserved since 27 March 2025, with their content carried by ISO/IEC/IEEE 24748-7 and 24748-8; the review standard is notable for naming a separate "Software Requirements and Architecture Review" alongside the hardware-shaped sequence ([IEEE 15288.2](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/15288.2/5705); [IEEE 15288.1](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/15288.1/5704/)). Its criteria are paywalled and were not read.

ECSS runs the same ladder with one inversion. Alternative system architectures are elaborated in Phase A and the concept is selected at PRR; the requirements are frozen afterwards, at SRR in Phase B, which reverses NASA's SRR-then-MDR order. "The product tree shall be established at start of phase B and finalized not later than PDR", and CDR releases the final design ([ECSS-M-ST-10C](https://ecss.nl/wp-content/uploads/standards/ecss-m/ECSS-M-ST-10C31July2008.pdf)). ECSS is also the only source in this set with a "shall" for separately producing a functional and a physical architecture and for justifying them in a design justification file, which makes recorded rationale normative ([ECSS-E-ST-10C Rev.1](https://ecss.nl/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/ECSS-E-ST-10C-Rev.1%2815February2017%29.pdf)). The phase text was read from the superseded 2008 issue; the active Rev.1 of 2009 was not opened.

GAO supplies the thresholds and most of the field data. Its three knowledge points ask for technologies near final form in a relevant environment and a completed PDR at development start, at least **90% of design drawings released** and a system-level integrated prototype at CDR, and manufacturing processes in statistical control at production ([GAO-02-701](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-02-701.pdf); [GAO-19-336SP](https://www.gao.gov/products/GAO-19-336SP)). An exploratory analysis of 15 and then 17 production programmes found "significantly lower cost and schedule growth" for those that completed the practices, without published effect sizes ([GAO-18-360SP](https://gao.gov/assets/gao-18-360sp-highlights.pdf)). Since 2022 GAO has reframed the model around iterative cycles, minimum viable products and a digital thread without relaxing technology maturity: the 2026 assessment says leading companies start only with technologies that will be mature, for example TRL 7, and finds that 18 of 40 middle-tier programmes began with immature ones ([GAO-23-106222](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106222); [GAO-26-108457 report](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-108457.pdf); [GAO-26-108457 summary](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108457)). The opposite failure is equally documented. The Constellation-class frigate began construction before its design was complete, passed its gate on a metric that "hinges largely on the quantity—rather than quality—of completed design documents", and its lead ship was forecast 36 months late ([GAO-24-106546](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-106546)).

The systems engineering community's own reformers change what a gate decides; they do not remove gates. Boehm and Lane call schedule-based and event-based reviews "risk-prone" (the PDR held "whether we have a design or not") and substitute feasibility evidence, "provided by the developer and validated by independent experts", as a first-class deliverable ([Boehm and Lane 2009](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2009/systemengr/9021WednesdayTrack7Boehm.pdf); [ICSM sample](https://ptgmedia.pearsoncmg.com/images/9780321808226/samplepages/0321808223.pdf)). Set-based concurrent engineering delays narrowing while exploring more broadly at the start ([Sobek, Ward and Liker](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/toyotas-principles-of-setbased-concurrent-engineering/)). The Defense Innovation Board concluded that "Software is never done" and that DoD manages software like hardware ([DIB 2019](https://media.defense.gov/2019/May/01/2002126690/-1/-1/0/SWAP%20EXECUTIVE%20SUMMARY.PDF)). These are three separate claims: gates are run on schedule or document count instead of evidence; requirements-then-architecture sequencing is wrong; and a whole-system baseline is the wrong unit of commitment for software. None argues for less architecture knowledge before commitment. Two holes remain in what was found. No opened source defines model-maturity criteria per gate under MBSE, and no peer-reviewed systems engineering study measuring early against deferred architecture baselining was located, and the search for one was cut short.

## The software canon asks for few drivers, one evidenced architecture and frozen interfaces

The founding texts say what must be fixed before work fans out. Parnas's modularizations "describe the design decisions which must be made before the work on independent modules can begin", and decomposition should start from the "design decisions which are likely to change" ([Parnas 1971](https://prl.khoury.northeastern.edu/img/p-tr-1971.pdf)). Parnas and Clements add that the module guide is not enough: "Once a module interface has been specified, its implementation can be carried out as an independent task" ([Parnas and Clements 1986](https://userweb.cs.txstate.edu/~rp31/papers/Fake_it.pdf)). Conway places "choice of a preliminary system concept" before the organisation of the design effort because "the very act of organizing a design team means that certain design decisions have already been made" ([Conway 1968](https://www.melconway.com/Home/Committees_Paper.html)). Garlan and Shaw list what the architecture level decides: gross organisation and global control structure, protocols for communication, synchronisation and data access, assignment of functionality to elements, physical distribution, scaling and performance ([Garlan and Shaw 1994](https://userweb.cs.txstate.edu/~rp31/papers/intro_softarch.pdf)). Perry and Wolf make rationale a first-class element so that erosion and drift can be resisted ([Perry and Wolf 1992](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~andre/informatics223s2017/perrywolf.pdf)). None of them asks for complete requirements first. Parnas and Clements call the ordered process an idealisation to be documented as if followed, and Conway says the first design "is almost never the best possible". All of this is argument and worked example, not field data.

Boehm gives the most explicit answer in the canon. The LCO package must contain a "Top-level definition of at least one feasible architecture" and an "Identification of infeasible architecture options". The LCA package must contain a chosen architecture elaborated by increment, with COTS, reuse, domain-architecture and style choices made and "Architecture evolution parameters" stated, and it must show "All major risks resolved or covered by risk management plan" ([Boehm 2000](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA382590.pdf)). One question decides the gate: if the product is built to the specified architecture, will it support the operational concept, satisfy the requirements and finish within the planned budget and schedule? If not, the package is reworked. Boehm's metaphor is that LCO is getting engaged and LCA is getting married, and "if you marry your architecture in haste, you and your stakeholders will repent at leisure". LCA binds iterative projects too: "Evolutionary or incremental development with no life cycle architecture" is listed as a hazardous look-alike of the spiral model. The level of detail is itself risk-driven: "If it's risky to not specify precisely, DO specify" (hardware-software interfaces) and "If it's risky to specify precisely, DO NOT specify" (GUI layout). His cautionary case runs the other way from the usual one: a prematurely specified one-second response time drove a custom architecture estimated near $100 million, and prototyping later showed that four seconds satisfied users about 90% of the time, which cut the estimate to about $30 million ([Boehm 2000](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA382590.pdf)). The Incremental Commitment Spiral Model keeps this content and turns the rationale into independently validated evidence, with risk-based outcomes at each review: proceed, do more work, or adjust scope and discontinue ([ICSM sample](https://ptgmedia.pearsoncmg.com/images/9780321808226/samplepages/0321808223.pdf)). Boehm's rule is therefore not "architecture before code". It is "no escalation of staff and funding past LCA without a chosen, evidenced architecture"; before that point code, prototypes and architecture grow together.

RUP adopted the anchor points as its phase gates ([Boehm 2000](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA382590.pdf)) and sharpened one of them: the architecture must execute. The Lifecycle Architecture milestone asks "Is the architecture stable?" and "Does the executable demonstration show that the major risk elements have been addressed and credibly resolved?", and expects a use-case model "at least 80% complete", a Software Architecture Description and "an executable architectural prototype"; a project that fails it "may be aborted or considerably re-thought" ([Rational TP026B](https://www.inf.ed.ac.uk/teaching/courses/seoc/2007_2008/resources/bestpractices.pdf)). The 80% figure measures requirements breadth, not implementation. Kruchten's 4+1 paper offers the nearest thing to an operational exit criterion: after "2 or 3 iterations" the architecture is stable when "no new major abstractions are found" ([Kruchten 1995](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~gregor/teaching/papers/4+1view-architecture.pdf)).

The SEI literature fixes an order of methods around the architecture and insists that design starts from very little. The Quality Attribute Workshop runs "before the software architecture has been created"; ATAM applies once the architecture is developed; CBAM "begins where the ATAM concludes" ([QAW report](https://people.computing.clemson.edu/~johnmc/courses/cpsc875/resources/qaw.pdf); [SEI TN-005](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/library/sei-architecture-analysis-techniques-and-when-to-use-them/); [SAiP 2nd ed. 12.1](https://people.ece.ubc.ca/matei/EECE417/BASS/ch12lev1sec1.html)). "It does not take many requirements in order for design to begin", and the architectural drivers "should be fewer than ten" ([SAiP 2nd ed. 7.1](https://people.ece.ubc.ca/matei/EECE417/BASS/ch07lev1sec1.html)). The same authors hold that the earliest decisions "are the most difficult to get correct and the hardest to change later", partly because the architecture becomes the work breakdown structure, after which "it becomes almost impossible, for managerial and business reasons, to modify it" ([SAiP 2nd ed. 2.4](https://people.ece.ubc.ca/matei/EECE417/BASS/ch02lev1sec4.html)). ADD 3.0 ties the amount of design to a stated "design purpose" (proposal, exploratory prototype or development), and its worked cases settle overall structure and technology selection first, then structures for primary functionality, then individual quality-attribute scenarios ([Cervantes and Kazman, sample](https://ptgmedia.pearsoncmg.com/images/9780134390789/samplepages/9780134390789.pdf)). An SEI report places the methods on the RUP calendar: QAW in Inception, ADD in the first Elaboration iteration, ATAM and CBAM late in Elaboration ([Kazman et al. 2004](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA455624.pdf)). These are prescriptive methods refined in field use and supported by case reports, not comparative trials.

Two lines of work undercut any strict ordering of requirements and architecture. Nuseibeh's Twin Peaks holds that requirements-first yields "artificially frozen requirements documents" and architecture-first yields "systems artificially constrained by their architecture", and prescribes concurrent, progressively detailed development of both ([Nuseibeh 2001](https://www.cin.ufpe.br/~straw01/epapers/paper05/paper05.html)). The one empirical study in this group, a grounded theory from interviews with **90 practitioners**, characterises architecturally significant requirements ([Chen, Ali Babar and Nuseibeh 2013](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/64499)); a secondary summary reports that such requirements are often vaguely expressed and "tend to be initially neglected" ([Wikipedia, Architecturally significant requirements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecturally_significant_requirements)). If that holds, a gate of the form "significant requirements identified before design" is weaker in practice than it reads on paper.

From 2004 the canon recasts architecture as a set of decisions with rationale ([Jansen and Bosch 2005](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/software-architecture-as-a-set-of-architectural-design-decisions)), which turns "what must be decided when" into a managed list. Zimmermann's reusable guidance models record "the project phase in which an issue typically is resolved" for each recurring issue; his SOA model holds "500 such recurring issues" and was applied on "more than 10 projects" ([Zimmermann 2011](https://www.infoq.com/articles/ieee-arch-decisions/)). It is the closest prior art to a reusable decision calendar, although the article does not publish the phase assignments. Hofmeister and colleagues, comparing five industrial methods, observe that architects do not follow the method sequence at all: activities "proceed in small leaps and bounds", driven by a backlog that risk and milestone pressure keep reprioritising ([Hofmeister et al. 2005](https://asw.inf.uniroma3.it/altrui/hofmeister2005.pdf)).

The practitioner frameworks differ in how much sequence they impose. TOGAF's method is the most artefact-gated: a sponsor, governance and principles before any architecture work, a signed Statement of Architecture Work, stakeholder-reviewed domain architectures with gap analyses, a roadmap with transition architectures and a build-or-buy orientation per gap, and an Architecture Contract before implementation ([Preliminary Phase](https://pubs.opengroup.org/togaf-standard/adm/chap02.html); [Phase A](https://pubs.opengroup.org/togaf-standard/adm/chap03.html); [Phase E](https://pubs.opengroup.org/togaf-standard/adm/chap09.html); [Phase G](https://pubs.opengroup.org/togaf-standard/adm/chap11.html)). Rozanski and Woods state that architecture definition "starts early in the project lifecycle, when scope and requirements are often still unclear" ([Rozanski and Woods, ch. 7](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/software-systems-architecture/9780132906135/ch07.html)). arc42 and C4 specify content and are silent on timing in the pages opened ([arc42](https://arc42.org/overview); [C4](https://c4model.com/)). Product-line engineering moves the commitment gate up one level: before the first product is derived there must be a scope and a product-line architecture with documented variation points, mechanisms and an instantiation process, yet scope "can be refined during the initial establishment of the product line or opportunistically" ([SAiP 2nd ed. 14.3](https://people.ece.ubc.ca/matei/EECE417/BASS/ch14lev1sec3.html); [SAiP 2nd ed. 14.4](https://people.ece.ubc.ca/matei/EECE417/BASS/ch14lev1sec4.html)). On the standards side, **ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207:2017 was withdrawn on 29 April 2026 and replaced by a 2026 edition**; it shares process purposes and outcomes with 15288 and prescribes no life cycle model ([ISO catalogue](https://www.iso.org/standard/63712.html)). The variable this canon leaves open is the size of the gate package: every source makes it depend on risk, scale or scope, and none fixes it numerically in the material verified.

## Deferral theory keeps a short early list and puts a price on the rest

The dominant rule in the agile and lean literature is that up-front architecture effort should be proportional to engineering risk. Fairbanks states it as "Architecture efforts should be commensurate with the risk of failure", with a stopping test (does the design overcome the identified failure risks?) and a mechanism for agile teams: iteration zero begins mitigating risks such as the choice of architectural style, after which "risks are pushed onto the backlog" ([Fairbanks 2010, sample chapter](https://www.georgefairbanks.com/assets/jesa/Just_Enough_Software_Architecture__Fairbanks_2010-demo.pdf)). Boehm supplies the only quantified version. COCOMO II's Architecture and Risk Resolution factor "was calibrated along with 22 others to 161 project data points", and the analysis found rework to be "an exponential function of project size": a 10 KSLOC project pays a **14% rework penalty** between minimal and extremely thorough architecting, a 10,000 KSLOC project **91%**. For a 100 KSLOC project the cost-minimising investment is roughly **20% of effort**; rapid change moves it to about 10%, high failure cost moves it above 30%, and the optima are "relatively flat 'sweet regions' extending 5-10% to the left and right" ([Boehm et al. 2010](https://web.mst.edu/lib-circ/files/Special%20Collections/INCOSE2010/Architected%20Agile%20Solutions%20for%20Software%20Reliant%20Systems.pdf)). Three cautions apply. The journal abstract reports 18% and 92% as cost differences for small and very large projects, and the accessible text does not show whether that is a different measure or a revision ([Boehm, Valerdi and Honour 2008](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/syseng/v11y2008i3p221-234.html)). The curves are outputs of a cost model calibrated on a database that predates continuous delivery and cloud platforms, and nothing found recalibrates them. And the sweet-spot percentages often quoted for the smallest and largest sizes could not be verified, because the chapter that tabulates them is paywalled and two secondary readings conflict. The direction is robust; the absolute numbers are an era estimate.

"Last responsible moment" began as a lead-time calculation in lean construction: "we can add safety-time to that lead-time in order to determine the last responsible moment" ([Ballard and Zabelle 2000](http://p2sl.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/W010-Ballard_Zabelle-2000-Lean-Design-Process-Tools-and-Techniques-LCI-White-Paper-10.pdf)). The Poppendiecks generalised it to "the moment at which failing to make a decision eliminates an important alternative" and warned that beyond it "decisions are made by default" (as reproduced by [Atwood 2006](https://blog.codinghorror.com/the-last-responsible-moment/); the book chapter was not readable). Read in its original form, the rule already places long-lead decisions early, and the later critiques restore that arithmetic under other names. Wirfs-Brock argues for "the most responsible moment" because wide-impact decisions take time to disseminate, others may be waiting, and nobody can reliably identify the last moment ([Wirfs-Brock 2011](https://wirfs-brock.com/rebecca/blog/2011/01/18/agile-architecture-myths-2-architecture-decisions-should-be-made-at-the-last-responsible-moment/)). Kruchten is reported as saying that for key architectural decisions the moment "is in fact very close to the beginning of the project" ([InfoQ 2010](https://www.infoq.com/news/2010/05/what-color-backlog)). Thoughtworks advocates the "latest responsible moment, which might in fact be up-front for some decisions" ([Thoughtworks Radar](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/techniques/evolutionary-architecture)), and Zimmermann's definition of ready for a decision includes that "its most responsible moment has arrived" ([Zimmermann 2023](https://ozimmer.ch/practices/2023/12/01/ADDefinitionOfReady.html)).

Real options theory gives deferral its economics and its limits. Sullivan's worked example has a restructuring that costs 1,600 against equally likely payoffs of 3,300 or 1,100: static net present value is 400, but waiting one period and investing only on the good outcome is worth 773, and the option's value rises with uncertainty ([Sullivan 2009](https://qconsf.com/sf2009/dl/qcon-sanfran-2009/slides/KevinSullivan_TheBusinessValueOfOptionsInSoftwareProductsProjectsAndProcesses.pdf)). Baldwin and Clark value a modular architecture as a portfolio of options net of the cost of creating the modularity ([Baldwin and Clark 2002](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/8944)). Matts and Maassen supply the practitioner form, "Options have value. Options expire. Never commit early unless you know why" ([Matts and Maassen 2007](https://www.infoq.com/articles/real-options-enhance-agility/)), and Hohpe the architect's form: architecture sells options, and options "aren't free" ([Hohpe 2016](https://architectelevator.com/architecture/architecture-options/)). Set-based concurrent engineering, widely cited as support for deciding late, is front-loaded in its source form: Toyota "considers a broader range of possible designs and delays certain decisions longer" while its suppliers multiply prototypes, and Liker describes the approach as front-loading the design process ([Sobek, Ward and Liker](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/toyotas-principles-of-setbased-concurrent-engineering/); [Ward et al. 1995](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/the-second-toyota-paradox-how-delaying-decisions-can-make-better-cars-faster/); [Liker 2021](https://www.lean.org/the-lean-post/articles/front-load-your-design-process-by-using-set-based-design/)). Late commitment is bought with early breadth. Reversibility ties these together. Bezos separates Type 1 "one-way doors", to be decided "methodically, carefully, slowly", from Type 2 decisions that small groups should make fast, and names a failure in each direction ([Bezos, 2015 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000119312516530910/d168744dex991.htm)). Fowler concludes that "one of an architect's most important tasks is to remove architecture" by eliminating irreversibility, and that YAGNI holds only where tests, continuous integration and refactoring keep the code malleable ([Fowler 2003](https://martinfowler.com/ieeeSoftware/whoNeedsArchitect.pdf); [Fowler, Yagni](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/Yagni.html)).

The evolutionary and continuous architecture school rejects "emergent" as a description of architecture; Parsons recalls "a very robust discussion about why that was a very bad name" ([Thoughtworks podcast 2022](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-us/insights/podcasts/technology-podcasts/rethinking-software-governance-reflecting-second-edition-building-evolutionary-architectures)). What it fixes first is not structure but the characteristics to protect and the automated checks on them, fitness functions "designed to have objective, quantifiable results" ([Ford, Parsons and Kua, excerpt](https://uio.no/studier/emner/matnat/ifi/ITLED4260/litteratur/building+evolutionary+architecture.pdf)). Continuous Architecture states the deferral rule most bluntly ("Delay design decisions until they are absolutely necessary") and pairs it with three conditions that make it safe: quality attributes drive the architecture, elements are small and loosely coupled, and teams are organised after the intended design ([Continuous Architecture principles](https://continuousarchitecture.com/continuous-architecture-principles/)). Fowler's staged advice is to start monolithic, because "even experienced architects working in familiar domains have great difficulty getting boundaries right at the beginning", and to design for about tenfold growth while planning to rewrite before a hundredfold ([Fowler, Monolith First](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/MonolithFirst.html); [Fowler, Sacrificial Architecture](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/SacrificialArchitecture.html)).

No mainstream agile method prescribes zero up-front architecture. In XP "The first iteration puts the architecture in place" (Beck as quoted by [Wake 2000](https://xp123.com/?p=203)). Disciplined Agile sets a "Proven Architecture" milestone one to three iterations into Construction, requiring "an end-to-end skeleton of working code that implements technically risky business requirements" ([Disciplined Agile milestones](https://www.pmi.org/disciplined-agile/agile/milestones)). Ambler's architecture envisioning takes "several days or a week or so" ([Ambler](https://agilemodeling.com/essays/initialArchitectureModeling.htm)), and Boehm reports a corporate "architected agile" process with "a 2-12 week architecting Sprint Zero" ([Boehm et al. 2010](https://web.mst.edu/lib-circ/files/Special%20Collections/INCOSE2010/Architected%20Agile%20Solutions%20for%20Software%20Reliant%20Systems.pdf)). SAFe requires that "some amount of architectural runway must exist going into the PI planning session" ([SAFe 2014](https://www.scaledagileframework.com/architectural-runway)). The Scrum Guide is silent, and Sprint 0 is practice, not prescription ([Scrum.org 2021](https://www.scrum.org/resources/blog/truth-about-sprint-zero-and-why-ken-hates-it)). LeSS is the outlier in rhetoric: it explicitly rejects the proposition that "It is important to have the architectural foundation before you implement anything else", yet it requires a small team to build the core before the group divides ([LeSS](https://less.works/less/technical-excellence/architecture-design)). The methods agree that the proof of an architecture is running end-to-end code on the risky path. They differ on whether a model precedes it and on whether the up-front step lasts hours or weeks.

The empirical base is thin, qualitative and consistent. Waterman, Noble and Allan's grounded theory (44 participants in 37 interviews) finds six forces and five strategies, and two results matter here: teams pursuing "emergent architecture" still fixed the technology stack and the highest-level styles up front, and big design up front was driven mainly by a process-bound customer, not by technical risk ([Colyer's summary](https://blog.acolyer.org/2015/06/22/how-much-up-front-a-grounded-theory-of-agile-architecture/); [thesis record](https://openaccess.wgtn.ac.nz/articles/thesis/Reconciling_agility_and_architecture_a_theory_of_agile_architecture/17007694)). The paper itself could not be opened. A survey of 72 developers at one IBM laboratory found 45% saying agile development should always focus on architecture, 50% when the project is complex and 5% never ([Falessi et al. 2010](https://art.torvergata.it/bitstream/2108/39088/4/2010_IEEESoftware-March2010_FCSCSD.pdf)). A systematic review found that of **130 published statements about agile and architecture, 91 rested on expert opinion** and 39 on empirical evidence ([Breivold et al. 2010](https://www.es.mdh.se/pdf_publications/1800.pdf)), and a later mapping study of 54 papers found costs and failure cases poorly described ([Yang, Liang and Avgeriou 2016](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/a-systematic-mapping-study-on-the-combination-of-software-archite)).

Architecture decision records are the practical device for incremental commitment: short, dated, immutable and supersedable ([Nygard 2011](https://www.cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions)). Zimmermann adds a significance test and a list of "core decisions" with an early most responsible moment, nominated at an ECSA 2020 working session: architectural style, technology stacks, integration approach and applicable regulations ([Zimmermann 2020](https://ozimmer.ch/practices/2020/09/24/ASRTestECSADecisions.html)). The evidence for the practice is early-stage. One IBM team recorded over 80 records in nearly two years and shifted about two-thirds of decision load from the architect to the team, although only about half the team recorded them routinely ([Keeling and Runde 2018](https://www.agilealliance.org/?p=8045735)). Repository mining finds adoption "still low", with about half of adopting repositories holding one to five records ([Buchgeher et al. 2023](https://research.jku.at/en/publications/using-architecture-decision-records-in-open-source-projects-an-ms/)), and in a set of 921 repositories and 5,800 records **about 63% were created already "accepted"**, with "predominantly small" correlations to code smells and issue-resolution time ([Santana Junior et al. 2026](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icsa-2026/icsa-2026-papers/34/Architecture-Decision-Records-Adoption-Impact-and-Developer-Engagement-in-Open-Sou)). That pattern suggests the record is mostly written after the decision, so its value as an instrument of deliberate deferral depends on team practice and not on the artefact.

Read closely, the deferral school's early list has been stable for twenty years and matches the plan-driven canon's: the quality attributes to protect, with targets; the technology stack and top-level style; the coarse decomposition that teams will be organised around; where data lives and its transactional scope; a deployment and operations model fit for continuous delivery; regulatory obligations; and anything irreversible or long-lead ([Continuous Architecture principles](https://continuousarchitecture.com/continuous-architecture-principles/); [Colyer's summary](https://blog.acolyer.org/2015/06/22/how-much-up-front-a-grounded-theory-of-agile-architecture/); [Zimmermann 2020](https://ozimmer.ch/practices/2020/09/24/ASRTestECSADecisions.html); [Bezos, 2015 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000119312516530910/d168744dex991.htm)). Each item is early for one of three reasons the theory predicts: it is expensive to reverse, it has a long lead time or many dependants, or it determines how everything later is evaluated. The school's distinctive contribution is to shrink each commitment to its minimum viable form: measurable targets instead of designs, a skeleton instead of a framework, module boundaries instead of service boundaries.

## Regulated domains fix criticality allocation first and verify architecture before detail

Every regime studied runs the same chain: a hazard, threat or risk analysis; requirements that carry a criticality attribute; an architecture that allocates requirements and criticality to elements; verification of that architecture against the requirements with bidirectional traceability; and only then refinement. What differs is whether the chain is enforced as a gate (functional safety, FDA review, type approval, the EU Cyber Resilience Act) or stated as an outcome (NIST's SSDF, OWASP SAMM, the NIST AI framework). One limit governs this whole section: the standards themselves are paywalled and were not read, so clause-level statements come from issuing bodies' abstracts, regulator documents and named vendor or academic summaries.

IEC 61508 sets the generic pattern. Edition 2 of Part 3 (2010) is still in force; edition 3 is at enquiry stage with publication expected about early 2027 ([IEC 61508-3:2010](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/5517); [61508 Association](https://61508.org/knowledge/standards-development/)). Software architecture design follows the software safety requirements specification and precedes detailed design, and Annex A grades architecture techniques by integrity level. A vendor's reproduction of the SIL 3 column shows the pull toward static structure: cyclic or time-triggered behaviour, static resource allocation and requirements-to-architecture traceability are highly recommended, while dynamic reconfiguration is not recommended ([Quantum Leaps mapping](https://www.state-machine.com/qpc/sas-qp_sc.html)). Combining elements raises systematic capability by at most one level, and "Redundant channels with identical software would have the software as a single point of failure" ([Meany 2019](https://ez.analog.com/ez-blogs/b/engineerzone-spotlight/posts/is-implementing-diverse-software-worth-the-effort)). Bishop's critique should travel with any citation of these tables: there is "no technical basis for the linkage between recommended software techniques and target software failure rate" ([Bishop 2005](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/545/2/SCSC_Newsletter_Software_SILs.pdf)).

Automotive and aviation make criticality an output of architecture. ISO 26262 proceeds from item definition and hazard analysis to safety goals with an ASIL, a functional safety concept allocated to preliminary architectural elements, then the technical safety concept and system architectural design ([ITEA SAFE D.6.b](https://itea4.org/project/workpackage/document/download/1998/D.6.b%20SAFE%20-%20Methodology%20and%20application%20rules%20Documentation%20Initial%20Documentation.pdf); [ISO 26262-4](https://www.iso.org/standard/68386.html)). An inherited ASIL can be lowered only by decomposition across "sufficiently independent" elements, and a software component is developed to the highest ASIL allocated to it unless coexistence criteria and freedom from interference are shown ([Presencis](https://cdn.presencis.com/regulations/iso-26262/article-4-asil-decomposition/); [AbsInt](https://www.absint.com/qualification/safety/)). Automotive SPICE 4.0 expresses the same order as a traceability chain and requires teams to "Document a rationale for the software architectural design decision" ([VDA QMC](https://vda-qmc.de/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Automotive-SPICE-PAM-v40.pdf)). In civil aviation the system architecture is developed together with the preliminary system safety assessment, and an item may receive a lower development assurance level than its function where independence is demonstrated through common-cause analysis ([Jama 2026](https://www.jamasoftware.com/blog/arp4761)); the levels and their justification go into a certification plan that needs FAA concurrence ([FAA AC 20-174](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_20-174.pdf)). DO-178C is objective-based and neutral about life cycle, with **71 objectives at Level A**, but its objectives require a developed architecture that is compatible with the high-level requirements, consistent, verifiable and target-compatible, and that "Software partitioning integrity is confirmed" ([Wikipedia, DO-178C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DO-178C); [AdaCore](https://learn-latest.adacore.com/booklets/adacore-technologies-for-airborne-software/chapters/summary.html)). Authority reviews check for it: the development review takes place when "typically at least 50 percent" (FAA, 2003) or "at least 75%" (EASA, 2011) of development data is complete, with the criterion that the software architecture is defined ([FAA Order 8110.49](https://www.faa.gov/documentLibrary/media/Order/FAA_Order_8110.49.pdf); [EASA CM-SWCEH-002](https://www.easa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/dfu/certification-docs-certification-memorandum-EASA-CM-SWCEH-002-Software-Aspects-of-Certification.pdf)). By implication, changing the architecture after levels are assigned can raise a level and invalidate completed evidence, which is why these allocations behave as one-way doors.

Medical-device rules add a prospective-documentation test. IEC 62304 requires Class B and C software to have an architecture, interface architecture, requirements on third-party components and a verified architecture; Class C adds the duty to "identify any segregation between SOFTWARE ITEMS that is necessary for RISK CONTROL" and verified detailed design ([OpenRegulatory](https://openregulatory.com/iec-62304-mapping-requirements-documents/); [Johner 2024](https://blog.johner-institute.com/?p=13715)). FDA's June 2023 guidance asks for architecture diagrams at both documentation levels and, at the Enhanced level, a design specification whose creation "is expected to have occurred as a prospective activity" ([FDA 2023](https://www.fda.gov/media/153781/download)). Its June 2025 cybersecurity guidance recommends threat modelling "throughout the design process" and a security architecture with four named views (global system, multi-patient harm, updateability and patchability, security use cases), and states that "For cyber devices, an SBOM is required" ([FDA 2025](https://www.hhs.gov/guidance/sites/default/files/hhs-guidance-documents/FDA/GUI00001825-final-PremarketCybersecurity-2025.pdf)). Those views push three decisions early: the update path, the blast radius across devices and patients, and every external connection.

Rail and industrial security show the same ordering with their own emphases. Under EN 50128 the architecture and design phase produces architecture, design and interface specifications together with integration test specifications and a verification report, so the architecture is not finished until the tests that will prove its integration are specified ([LDRA](https://ldra.com/wp-content/uploads/ldra/Implementing-EN5012X-with-the-LDRA-tool-suite-v1.pdf)). Its successor, EN 50716:2023, "expands on development life cycle models" to cover iterative and agile approaches ([Parasoft](https://parasoft.com/blog/en-50716-impact-on-rail-digitalization/)). IEC 62443-4-1 places the threat model in the requirements practice, ahead of the secure-by-design practice ([ISA-62443-4-1 contents](https://www.isa.org/getmedia/99afef02-46b1-4d75-9b46-4c4531fd1c34/isa-62443-4-1-toc.pdf)), and IEC 62443-3-2 requires the system to be partitioned into zones and conduits with a target security level for each ([IEC 62443-3-2](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/30727)). Zone partitioning does for security what ASIL and IDAL allocation do for safety.

General security guidance is far less phase-ordered, and law is what gives it gate character. NIST's SSDF asks teams to determine "how the software's design and architecture should mitigate those risks", to track "security requirements, risks, and design decisions" and to have the design reviewed by someone "not involved with the design", while stating that the order of practices "is not intended to imply the sequence of implementation" ([NIST SP 800-218](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-218.pdf)). OWASP ASVS 4.0.3 asks for "threat modeling for every design change or sprint planning" ([ASVS 4.0.3](https://github.com/OWASP/ASVS/blob/v4.0.3/4.0/en/0x10-V1-Architecture.md)). The EU Cyber Resilience Act converts this into obligation: the cybersecurity risk assessment must be taken into account during the "planning, design, development, production, delivery and maintenance phases", and Annex I requires secure-by-default configuration, a limited attack surface, an update mechanism and data minimisation, none of which can be added by late hardening ([CRA Art. 13, reproduction](https://www.european-cyber-resilience-act.com/Cyber_Resilience_Act_Article_13.html); [Annex I, workshop deck](https://www.ds.dk/media/pzmatj0m/essential-cybersecurity-requirements-and-definitions.pdf)). Privacy law fixes its trigger even earlier. GDPR Article 25 attaches the design duty to the moment the means of processing are determined ([GDPR Art. 25](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-25-gdpr/)); the EDPB reads "means" to "range from the general to the detailed design elements", architecture included, counts procurement as part of that moment, and calls late change "challenging and costly" ([EDPB Guidelines 4/2019](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_201904_dataprotection_by_design_and_by_default_v2.0_en.pdf)). Article 35 requires an impact assessment "prior to the processing" containing "a systematic description of the envisaged processing operations", which cannot be written without at least a logical architecture and a data-flow model ([GDPR Art. 35](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-35-gdpr/)).

The EU AI Act adds architectural obligations that classic software standards do not name. High-risk systems "shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system"; oversight measures must be "identified and built, when technically feasible, into the high-risk AI system" before it is placed on the market; robustness may rely on "technical redundancy solutions"; and data governance covers "the relevant design choices" for training, validation and test sets ([Art. 12](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/12/); [Art. 14](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/14/); [Art. 15](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/15/); [Art. 10](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/10/)). NIST's voluntary framework says risk management should ideally "start with the Plan and Design function" ([NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)). For AI embedded in an already regulated product, two regimes will stack: the sector standard's architecture gates plus the Act's design obligations.

Taken together, the regimes treat seven kinds of decision as effectively irreversible. The term and the grouping are a synthesis, not the standards' language: criticality allocation (FDAL and IDAL, ASIL and its decomposition, SIL, IEC 62304 class, security level per zone); partitioning, segregation and freedom from interference; redundancy, diversity and independence; trust boundaries and external interfaces; the timing and resource model; update and logging paths; and personal-data topology. Most of the standards do not mandate a waterfall. DO-178C requires no specific life cycle, EN 50128 says only that "a lifecycle model for the development of software shall be selected", and NIST SP 800-160 allows processes to run "recursively, iteratively, concurrently, sequentially, or in parallel" ([AdaCore](https://learn-latest.adacore.com/booklets/adacore-technologies-for-airborne-software/chapters/summary.html); [LDRA](https://ldra.com/wp-content/uploads/ldra/Implementing-EN5012X-with-the-LDRA-tool-suite-v1.pdf); [NIST SP 800-160v1r1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-160v1r1.pdf)). They constrain the dependency order of evidence for each released baseline. The published agile adaptations all keep the same things outside the sprints. A railway "Scrum for Safety" case keeps the system requirements and hazard analysis as up-front inputs, adds independent verifier, validator and assessor roles, and reports **verification and validation taking 75% of time** with "unpredictable" integration rework ([Barbareschi et al. 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11219-022-09593-2)). The cost of iteration under these regimes is re-verification, and architecture-affecting change is the expensive case.

The academic critiques agree that the phase and technique prescriptions are consensus practice with little validation. Graydon and Holloway observe that "little is known about whether such standards" work ([Graydon and Holloway 2015](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20150018918)); Rushby notes that the argument behind aircraft software guidance "is generally not formulated explicitly, nor recorded" ([Rushby 2011](https://www.csl.sri.com/users/rushby/papers/emsoft11.pdf)). Leveson and Thomas give the constructive version: hazard analysis "can be started in early concept analysis" so that it shapes the architecture instead of auditing it ([STPA Handbook](https://psas.scripts.mit.edu/home/get_file.php?name=STPA_handbook.pdf)).

Many of these instruments are moving, which matters for anyone planning a product for 2027 or later.

## Cloud-era practice sorts decisions by reversal cost and defers capacity

The cloud, DevOps and startup literature converges on one sorting rule: deliberate in proportion to the cost of reversal, and treat the architect's job as turning one-way doors into two-way doors. AWS wrote the vocabulary into its review process, which places reviews "early on in the design phase to avoid one-way doors that are difficult to change, and then before the go-live date" ([AWS Well-Architected, review process](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/framework/the-review-process.html)). The one-way doors that authors keep naming are few. Externally consumed API contracts come first: "APIs are forever... we'd only have one chance to get it right" ([Vogels 2016](https://www.allthingsdistributed.com/2016/03/10-lessons-from-10-years-of-aws.html)), and Stripe reports maintaining compatibility with every API version since 2011 ([Leach 2017](https://stripe.com/blog/api-versioning)). The primary data model follows ([Kleppmann 2017](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/designing-data-intensive-applications/9781491903063/ch02.html)), with tenant and partition keys the costly part in the case reports below, then service boundaries drawn before the domain is understood ([Newman 2015](https://samnewman.io/blog/2015/04/07/microservices-for-greenfield/)), each added technology, budgeted by McKinley at "about three innovation tokens" per company ([McKinley 2015](https://mcfunley.com/choose-boring-technology)), and the security and reliability posture, since "Reliability is primarily an emergent property of the design of your system" and is hard to "bolt on" ([Adkins et al. 2020, ch. 4](https://google.github.io/building-secure-and-reliable-systems/raw/ch04.html)). Reversibility in this literature is manufactured, not given: schema change became routine once migration tooling existed ([Sadalage and Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/articles/evodb.html)), and Stripe changes live data models with a four-step dual-write procedure ([Xu 2017](https://stripe.com/blog/online-migrations)). Early investment therefore goes into the mechanisms that make later change cheap (tests, a pipeline, migrations, modularity) and not into presumptive capability. Nearly all of this is practitioner opinion.

The hyperscaler frameworks place very little before the first workload. Microsoft separates "environment design areas" (billing and tenant, identity, resource organisation, network topology), whose decisions "affect the platform foundation", from compliance design areas that are "the focus for iterative refinement" ([Azure landing zone design areas](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cloud-adoption-framework/ready/landing-zone/design-areas)). AWS recommends a multi-account structure but tells a team that is still experimenting that "you don't need to consider these best practices until you begin planning for your first few production workloads" ([AWS, multiple accounts](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/whitepapers/latest/organizing-your-aws-environment/organizing-your-aws-environment.html)). The maturity ladders are sequencing models that warn against buying later levels early: CNCF advises building "the thinnest viable platform layer" and paying "the price of standardization only when necessary later on" ([CNCF platforms paper](https://tag-app-delivery.cncf.io/whitepapers/platforms/); [CNCF platform maturity model](https://tag-app-delivery.cncf.io/whitepapers/platform-eng-maturity-model/)). No framework publishes evidence that reviews at its stated milestones improve outcomes.

Google's SRE corpus moves the operability gate steadily earlier. The production readiness review came first, and its admitted weakness was timing: "the SRE engagement starts very late in the development lifecycle" ([SRE book ch. 32](https://sre.google/sre-book/evolving-sre-engagement-model/)). The fixed points that survive are SLOs before general availability and an error-budget policy agreed with product owners ([SRE Workbook ch. 18](https://sre.google/workbook/engagement-model/)), with the concession that a first SLO "doesn't have to be correct" ([SRE Workbook ch. 2](https://sre.google/workbook/implementing-slos/)). The same texts license a deliberately low target early: "100% is probably never the right reliability target", and Google set a lower availability target for YouTube than for enterprise products because feature velocity mattered more ([SRE book ch. 3](https://sre.google/sre-book/embracing-risk/)).

DORA's research programme is the strongest quantitative support in this body of work, and what it measures is behavioural. The architectural capability is whether a team can change its design without outside permission, test on demand without an integrated environment and deploy independently of the services it depends on, and DORA is explicit that "It's possible to achieve these outcomes with mainframe technologies" ([DORA, loosely coupled teams](https://dora.dev/capabilities/loosely-coupled-teams/)). A well-factored monolith with one pipeline meets all three while there is one team. The 2025 report, from nearly 5,000 respondents, adds that "AI doesn't fix a team; it amplifies what's already there" and that AI adoption still has "a negative relationship with software delivery stability" ([Harvey and DeBellis 2025](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report)). The inference for sequencing is that test automation and pipeline quality now belong earlier than the pre-2023 startup literature assumed.

Organisation design supplies the trigger for the first hard boundary. Conway's observation that organising a design team already commits design decisions ([Conway 1968](https://www.melconway.com/Home/Committees_Paper.html)) means that with one team there is no team-driven reason for a service boundary, and the first team split is the first point at which a module boundary becomes hard to move. Domain-driven bounded contexts are the recommended unit ([Fowler, Conway's Law](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/ConwaysLaw.html)), and Wardley's doctrine gives the build-or-buy rule: build in-house only what is still evolving and rent what has become product or commodity ([Wardley, Doctrine](https://medium.com/wardleymaps/doctrine-8bb0015688e5)). These are practitioner frameworks; no empirical evidence for cognitive-load limits or the inverse Conway manoeuvre was found.

The peer-reviewed startup evidence is thin, qualitative and survivor-biased, and it is consistent. A grounded theory of 13 startups reports "the general lack of architectural design, substituted by high-level mock-ups and low-precision diagrams", notes that "the use of standards and frameworks reduces the need for a formal architectural design", and records two early choices interviewees later regretted: skipping a framework and a poor initial data structure ([Giardino et al. 2016](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.09438)). A case survey of 84 startups finds that they minimise risk with well-known, stable technologies and that architecture debt becomes a named challenge at growth and maturity: "Correcting simple architecture mistakes that could have been trivial to fix early, can take weeks now" ([Klotins et al., progression model](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.07106)). In 86 startups, **architecture debt was almost absent before release and rose during stabilisation and growth**, and team size was the most influential factor, with teams of nine or more reporting more debt ([Klotins et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.12434)). Besker and colleagues propose a "Good Enough Level" of debt per phase, with a stabilisation refactoring and then a growth refactoring, architectural refactoring included, before scaling and hiring ([Besker et al. 2018](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/507561/file/507561_Fulltext.pdf)). The much-quoted Startup Genome figure, "74% of high growth internet startups fail due to premature scaling", comes from self-reported data on more than 3,200 startups with no stated definition of failure and should not be cited as causal ([Marmer et al. 2012](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/startup-genomereport2-whystartupsfailv2/49342055)). The usable timing rule is that debt taken before first release is cheap and the bill arrives between first release and growth, driven by team size and customer count.

Company case reports sharpen which early decisions were regretted. They concern the data model, not the timing of services. Slack "was architected with the assumption that almost all data is particular to a single workspace", and undoing that assumption meant updating "thousands of APIs, database queries, and permissions checks" over "almost two years" ([Hoffman and Demmer 2024](https://slack.engineering/unified-grid-how-we-re-architected-slack-for-our-largest-customers/)). Notion ran one Postgres database through "five years and four orders of magnitude of growth" and lists as lessons "Shard earlier" and "Introduce a combined primary key instead of a separate partition key" ([Fidalgo 2021](https://www.notion.com/blog/sharding-postgres-at-notion)). AWS's SaaS guidance says the same in advance: "Bind user identity to tenant identity", make tenant context "a first-class construct", and treat the physical isolation model as a per-service choice that can change ([AWS SaaS Lens, principles](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/saas-lens/general-design-principles.html); [AWS SaaS Lens, silo, pool and bridge](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/saas-lens/silo-pool-and-bridge-models.html)). On services the cases point the other way. Amazon, Twitter, Uber and Shopify describe their monoliths as right for their time ([Vogels and Gray 2006](https://queue.acm.org/detail.cfm?id=1142065); [Westeinde 2019](https://shopify.engineering/deconstructing-monolith-designing-software-maximizes-developer-productivity)), while the reversals describe decomposition adopted ahead of an organisational need: Segment grew to "over 140 services" with "3 full-time engineers spending most of their time just keeping the system alive" ([Noonan 2018](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/blog/developers/best-practices/goodbye-microservices)), and Istio's control plane was consolidated because its microservices did not match "organizational reality" ([Box 2020](https://istio.io/latest/blog/2020/istiod/)). Uber's later account, after grouping about 2,200 services into 70 domains, is that "in small organizations, the operational benefit likely does not offset the increase in architectural complexity" ([Gluck 2020](https://www.uber.com/blog/microservice-architecture/)). Shopify's follow-up is the counterweight to easy monolith-first optimism: eighteen months into modularisation, "Almost no component has a complete, strong boundary yet" ([Müller 2020](https://shopify.engineering/shopify-monolith)). All of these are self-reported, selected for interest and silent on counterfactuals.

For AI products the durable decisions are not the model. Sculley and colleagues observed that "Only a small fraction of real-world ML systems is composed of the ML code" ([Sculley et al. 2015](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2015/file/86df7dcfd896fcaf2674f757a2463eba-Paper.pdf)), and Google's rules of machine learning are an explicit sequence: launch without machine learning if need be, design metrics first, then "Keep the first model simple and get the infrastructure right" ([Zinkevich](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/guides/rules-of-ml)). Practitioners building on language models advise "No GPUs before PMF" and "Start with prompting, evals, and data collection", on the ground that models "are likely to be the least durable component in the system" ([Yan et al. 2024](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/what-we-learned-from-a-year-of-building-with-llms-part-iii-strategy/)). The practical one-way door is failing to capture inputs, outputs and feedback from the first user, because that data cannot be recreated. These sources date from 2023 and 2024; nothing later on agent architectures was searched.

Technical due diligence is practitioner lore backed by one large data set. A diligence consultancy reports that "we've seen great architecture and terrible code succeed whereas terrible architecture and great code rarely is successful" ([Abbott 2018](https://akfpartners.com/growth-blog/technical-due-diligence-best-practices)). GitLab's published acquisition process puts an architecture walkthrough in early diligence, before a term sheet, and asks for a technical bill of materials listing repositories, dependencies, database schemas and security reports ([GitLab Handbook](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/acquisitions/acquisition-process/)), and Larson's checklist adds whether the implementation is first-party, what certifications exist, how the company makes technology decisions and what it costs to run per dollar of revenue ([Larson 2023](https://lethain.com/engineering-in-mergers-and-acquisition/)). The data set is Black Duck's 2026 audit of 947 codebases, 197 of them for M&A transactions, in which **68% contained open-source licence conflicts** ([Black Duck 2026](https://www.blackduck.com/resources/analyst-reports/open-source-security-risk-analysis.html)). No peer-reviewed study linking architectural debt to valuation was found, nothing opened describes technical diligence at seed or Series A, and the common advice that the first enterprise deal triggers SOC 2 could not be sourced.

Only the prototype and post-product-market-fit rows have peer-reviewed support, all of it qualitative or correlational. The MVP row rests on vendor guidance, one statute and company case reports, and it is the one row where the sources pull apart: the empirical studies describe companies that deferred nearly everything and survived, while the case reports name a handful of decisions those companies later wished they had settled.

## The direction is supported; almost every circulating number is not

Grades in this section use four levels: strong (several independent data sets, consistent direction, plausible causal design), moderate (real data and sizeable samples, but observational, single-context or self-reported), weak (small or opaque samples, expert opinion, model-derived or single case) and folklore (untraceable, misattributed or illustrative numbers circulated as data).

The cost-of-change curve is the least secure pillar. Nearly all citations of a hundredfold escalation trace to Boehm's 1981 book, which drew on four 1970s sources from TRW, GTE, IBM and Bell Labs ([Menzies et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.04886)). Boehm and Basili later qualified it themselves: "often 100 times more expensive" after delivery, but "more like 5:1" for small non-critical systems, with good architecture lowering the factor for large ones ([Boehm and Basili 2001](https://www.cs.umd.edu/projects/SoftEng/ESEG/papers/82.78.pdf)). An expert workshop put severe defects near 100:1 on the strength of two contributed ratios and non-severe defects near 2:1 ([Shull et al. 2002](http://www.cs.umd.edu/~mvz/pub/eworkshop02.pdf)). The two most circulated modern attributions do not hold up. The "IBM Systems Sciences Institute" ratios trace to internal course notes cited by a 1987 textbook ([The Register 2021](https://www.theregister.com/2021/07/22/bugs_expense_bs/)), and the "NIST 2002" table either republishes older literature with assumptions or is labelled "(Example Only)" ([NIST Planning Report 02-3](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/director/planning/report02-3.pdf)). NASA's often-cited range of 29x to more than 1,500x at operations takes its upper bound from a modelled hypothetical satellite; the two methods using real project data gave 29x (spacecraft modifications) and 157x to 186x (an aircraft programme) ([Stecklein et al. 2004](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20100036670/downloads/20100036670.pdf)). The largest modern test, **171 projects and about 47,000 defect logs**, found no median growth ratio above 3.0 ([Menzies et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.04886)). That study covers small, disciplined projects before release, does not analyse severity, and explicitly exempts the "baseline architecture" from its conclusion. The defensible residue is conditional: escalation is driven by a minority of severe, cross-cutting changes in large systems. No study found measures that minority directly, and every data set here measures defect fixes, not the reversal of a decision.

Evidence on the return from up-front engineering agrees in direction and is observational throughout. Honour's interviews on 48 programmes yield an optimum systems engineering effort of about 14.4% of programme cost, but the weights were tuned, the optimum ranges from about 8% to 19% with programme characteristics, and the effort correlates with cost and schedule compliance and **not with technical quality** ([Honour 2013](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2013/system/W16280_Honour.pdf); [Honour 2010](https://www.incose.org/wp-content/uploads/legacy/presentation/IS2010-pdf/11-4-2.pdf)). The SEI and NDIA survey of 148 projects finds product-architecture capability associated with project performance at gamma +0.41 and trade studies at +0.38, with the overall relationship stronger on higher-challenge projects (+0.62 against +0.34); predictor and outcome are both self-reported ([Elm and Goldenson 2012](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA610517.pdf)). The Gruhl chart of NASA overrun against definition spending has no locatable primary source ([Mink 2006](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2006/systems/Tuesday/mink.pdf)), and a National Research Council review lists among its references' findings "no discernable correlation between actual cost performance and percent of funds spent during Phase B formulation" ([NRC 2010](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/12946/chapter/8)). GAO's technology-maturity result is large (4.8% against 34.9% development cost growth) but the mature group is roughly five programmes, and programmes able to start with mature technology are less ambitious ([GAO-06-391](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-06-391.pdf)).

Code-archaeology and technical-debt studies show that architecture quality matters and say nothing about when it was decided. Files involved in architecture anti-patterns were more bug-prone and change-prone in all 19 projects studied, with size and architectural role not fully controlled ([Mo et al.](https://www.cs.drexel.edu/~yc349/papers/2019/tse2019.pdf)); architecturally connected file groups consumed 51 to 85% of maintenance effort in seven Apache projects ([Xiao et al. 2016](https://personal.stevens.edu/~lxiao6/papers/ICSE-16-Debt.pdf)). The widely quoted refactoring return is an ex ante projection in one project ([Kazman 2015](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/blog/a-case-study-in-locating-the-architectural-roots-of-technical-debt/)). Mozilla's deliberate redesign shows that architecture can be changed later by funded effort ([MacCormack, Rusnak and Baldwin 2006](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v52y2006i7p1015-1030.html)). In a three-organisation survey with 536 complete responses, 54% ranked bad architecture choices among the top three sources of debt ([Ernst et al. 2015](https://cs.unibg.it/esecfse_proceedings/fse15/p50-ernst.pdf)), and a five-company case study describes accumulation toward crisis points while arguing that some of it cannot be avoided ([Martini, Bosch and Chaudron 2015](https://mn.uio.no/ifi/english/people/aca/antonima/papers/InvestigatingATD.pdf)). Two further studies define the typical debt item as a decision that was right when made and wrong once the context moved; the most common item in one is "New Context, Old Architecture" ([Verdecchia et al. 2021](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/building-and-evaluating-a-theory-of-architectural-technical-debt-/); [Borowa, Zalewski and Kijas 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.14175)). That is evidence against the idea that deciding earlier or more thoroughly prevents architectural debt.

The organisational evidence is the best in the set. In a review of 142 studies, 70% of 50 firm-level studies support the mirroring of product and organisation structure against 28% of 18 open-collaboration studies, and ten cases of poorly performing unmirrored systems were "usually due to premature modularization" ([Colfer and Baldwin 2016](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/16-124_7ae90679-0ce6-4d72-9e9d-828872c7af49.pdf)). In Windows Vista, organisational metrics predicted failure-prone binaries with **86.2% precision and 84.0% recall**, better than churn, complexity or dependency metrics ([Nagappan, Murphy and Basili 2008](https://www.cs.umd.edu/users/basili/publications/proceedings/P125.pdf)). Finer-grained congruence effects are positive in two commercial projects, where over half of each month's new cross-team coordination needs involved people who were not interdependent the month before ([Cataldo and Herbsleb 2013](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10038372)), and null in 25 open-source projects ([Mauerer et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.08198)). DORA's 2017 finding that loosely coupled architectures and teams are the strongest predictor of continuous delivery rests on 3,200 snowball-sampled self-reports, and the construct ("we can deploy independently") sits close to the outcome ("we deploy often") ([State of DevOps 2017](https://dora.dev/publications/pdf/state-of-devops-2017.pdf); [Jones](https://blogs.accu.org/category/dora/)). Team structure is thus an early architectural decision with real data behind it, and one that the same data says will drift.

Several staples should be retired. The Standish success and failure rates did not reflect reality when tested against 5,457 forecasts from 1,211 projects ([Eveleens and Verhoef 2010](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/the-rise-and-fall-of-the-chaos-report-figures/)). Brooks withdrew "plan to throw one away" in 1995 on the ground that it assumes a waterfall process ([Brooks 1995, contents](https://ptgmedia.pearsoncmg.com/images/9780201835953/samplepages/0201835959.pdf); wording via a [secondary quotation](https://blog.safnet.com/2011/12/11/mythical_man-month_planning_for_change)). Field studies of decision-making show the prescribed methods are rarely what happens: in one company none of 15 architects' teams used a standard decision technique ([Dasanayake et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.00107)), and anchoring was the most frequent bias in a 12-practitioner study, linked to architectural lock-in ([Borowa, Zalewski and Kijas 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.14175)). Early commitment therefore carries a behavioural cost that the cost-of-change literature does not count. Practitioner belief runs ahead of all of this: 564 Microsoft engineers reported forming beliefs mainly from personal experience and least from research ([Devanbu, Zimmermann and Bird 2016](https://thomas-zimmermann.com/publications/files/devanbu-icse-2016.pdf)).

Five things survive a sceptical reading. Size and criticality moderate every effect, so the case for early architecture is weakest for small systems and strongest for large, multi-team, high-assurance ones. The costly class is narrow: most defects and most decisions are cheap to change late. Technology maturity and organisational partitioning are the two early factors with the best data. Architecture can be changed later by deliberate, funded effort, and some architectural debt is unavoidable because context shifts. And up-front engineering effort buys cost and schedule predictability, not a better product. The hole under all of it is that **no study, experimental or quasi-experimental, varies when architectural decisions are made while holding total effort constant**, and none compares early commitment against an iterative strategy in which the architecture is validated by early running code.

## The canons disagree on order, proof and the unit of commitment

The first disagreement is whether requirements or architecture come first, and it survives inside single frameworks. NASA baselines requirements at SRR and the architecture at MDR/SDR; ECSS selects the concept and architecture at PRR and freezes requirements afterwards at SRR ([NPR 7123.1D Ch. 5](https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayDir.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_7123_001D_&page_name=Chapter5); [ECSS-M-ST-10C](https://ecss.nl/wp-content/uploads/standards/ecss-m/ECSS-M-ST-10C31July2008.pdf)). Safety standards demand traceability from requirements to architecture, yet ISO 26262 needs a preliminary architecture before functional safety requirements can be allocated, and aviation derives the assurance level from the architecture's independence argument ([ITEA SAFE D.6.b](https://itea4.org/project/workpackage/document/download/1998/D.6.b%20SAFE%20-%20Methodology%20and%20application%20rules%20Documentation%20Initial%20Documentation.pdf); [Jama 2026](https://www.jamasoftware.com/blog/arp4761)). Twin Peaks and the concurrency language of 15288 reject the question ([Nuseibeh 2001](https://www.cin.ufpe.br/~straw01/epapers/paper05/paper05.html); [SEBoK, Generic Life Cycle Model](https://sebokwiki.org/wiki/Generic_Life_Cycle_Model)). The workable reading is that the standards fix a dependency order of evidence, not a calendar order of work.

The second is what passes a gate. DoD guidance and GAO count released drawings; NASA has questioned the 90% measure as a legacy standard, the Navy disputes that complete functional design is needed before contract award, and the frigate shows a count-based metric passing an unstable design ([GAO-20-405](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-20-405.pdf); [GAO-24-105503](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-105503); [GAO-24-106546](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-106546)). Boehm asks for independently validated feasibility evidence, RUP for an executable prototype, the agile methods for a running skeleton, the safety standards for a verification report with traces, and FDA for documentation shown to be prospective. These are different instruments for the same question, and they are not interchangeable: a model can pass where the skeleton would fail, and the reverse.

The third is whether to get early decisions right or to make them reversible. The SEI holds that the earliest decisions are "the most difficult to get correct and the hardest to change later" and Cervantes and Kazman that "architecture enables agility, and not the other way around" ([SAiP 2nd ed. 2.4](https://people.ece.ubc.ca/matei/EECE417/BASS/ch02lev1sec4.html); [Cervantes and Kazman, sample](https://ptgmedia.pearsoncmg.com/images/9780134390789/samplepages/9780134390789.pdf)). Fowler would shrink the set by removing irreversibility ([Fowler 2003](https://martinfowler.com/ieeeSoftware/whoNeedsArchitect.pdf)). Hohpe prices the alternative: options cost money, and "Minimizing switching costs may not be the most economical choice" ([Hohpe 2019](https://martinfowler.com/articles/oss-lockin.html)). The positions are complementary levers on one variable, the cost of late change, but they send budget to different places: analysis and evaluation in one case, tests, pipeline and migration tooling in the other. A related split runs through the startup literature. Ries holds that "Startups especially can benefit by using technical debt to experiment" ([Ries 2009](http://www.startuplessonslearned.com/2009/07/embrace-technical-debt.html)); Fowler holds that internal quality is not tradable for speed, while labelling his own estimate of when design pays off, "usually weeks not months", a conjecture ([Fowler 2011](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/TradableQualityHypothesis.html); [Fowler 2007](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/DesignStaminaHypothesis.html)).

The fourth is when to draw module and service boundaries. Fowler's monolith-first and Tilkov's "Don't start with a monolith" agree that boundaries are the expensive thing and differ on whether a team can know them at the start; Tilkov's own condition is that "you know the domain you're building for really, really well" ([Tilkov 2015](https://martinfowler.com/articles/dont-start-monolith.html)). Colfer and Baldwin's "premature modularization" failures support waiting; Shopify's incomplete boundaries, Conway's law and Parnas's advice to decompose around likely change support acting early. For defence programmes statute and policy settle it: a modular open systems approach is mandatory "to the maximum extent practicable", and the programme's approach is presented at Milestone B ([10 U.S.C. 4401](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/4401); [DoDI 5000.88](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/500088p.pdf)).

The fifth is the unit of commitment. The major-capability frameworks baseline a whole system. The DoD software pathway, RUP iterations and GAO's current leading practices commit to an increment, and GAO's own path from knowledge points in 2002 to iterative cycles in 2023 kept the demand for mature technology while shrinking the thing committed to ([GAO-02-701](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-02-701.pdf); [GAO-23-106222](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106222)). Agile methods add a version of the same dispute about foundations: LeSS rejects a foundation before features, SAFe requires runway before planning, and Boehm excludes evolutionary development without a life cycle architecture.

Four narrower conflicts are worth knowing. Technology-maturity thresholds differ by context without a reconciling statement: TRL 6 by PDR as GAO's benchmark for NASA, "relevant environment" in statute, which the DoD guidebook says covers both TRL 5 and TRL 6, and TRL 7 at start for leading companies in GAO's 2026 report ([GAO-20-405](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-20-405.pdf); [TRA Guidebook](https://www.cto.mil/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/TRA-Guide-Jun2023.pdf); [GAO-26-108457 report](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-108457.pdf)). The architecture canon claims that quality attributes are substantially determined by architecture, while Honour finds up-front effort unrelated to technical quality ([SAiP 2nd ed. 2.4](https://people.ece.ubc.ca/matei/EECE417/BASS/ch02lev1sec4.html); [Honour 2013](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2013/system/W16280_Honour.pdf)). Team structure is to be fixed before scaling according to Conway, SAFe and LeSS, yet coordination needs are volatile and Team Topologies says "Team boundaries shouldn't be fixed permanently" ([Team Topologies](https://teamtopologies.com/key-concepts)). And the "last responsible moment" dispute is mostly definitional once the original lead-time arithmetic is restored.

## Eleven threads worth pulling, starting with the missing timing study

**The missing timing study.** No study found timestamps architectural decisions against project phases or varies their timing with effort held constant. Three entry points exist. Menzies and colleagues explicitly leave the "baseline architecture" outside their null result ([Menzies et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.04886)). The Norwegian programme studied by Dingsøyr had two suppliers, one deciding much of its architecture during solution description and the other during construction, whose outcomes the paper does not compare quantitatively ([Dingsøyr et al. 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-017-9524-2)). And decision records in version control carry dates: the 921-repository set already exists ([Santana Junior et al. 2026](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icsa-2026/icsa-2026-papers/34/Architecture-Decision-Records-Adoption-Impact-and-Developer-Engagement-in-Open-Sou)). For a researcher this is the most open question in the field.

**Boehm's curves, verified and recalibrated.** The journal article (DOI 10.1002/sys.20096) and Table 10-4 of his chapter in *Making Software* hold the numbers that could not be read for this report ([chapter preview](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/making-software/9780596808310/ch10s02.html)). The percentages usually quoted for 10, 100 and 10,000 KSLOC, roughly 5%, 20% and 37%, are recalled and unverified here. The chapter also has an unread section titled "Does the Architecting Need to Be Done Up Front?". Nothing found recalibrates the model for continuous delivery, managed platforms or AI-assisted coding.

**Feasibility evidence as the gate deliverable.** Boehm's feasibility evidence description is the most transferable idea in the plan-driven canon, and it arguably maps onto what an acquirer's technical diligence asks a startup to show. The ICSM book's phase chapters and its appendix mapping to other standards were outside the publisher sample ([ICSM sample](https://ptgmedia.pearsoncmg.com/images/9780321808226/samplepages/0321808223.pdf)). The revision of ISO/IEC/IEEE 42030, the evaluation framework, opened in January 2026 and is worth watching for the same reason ([ISO 42030:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/73436.html)).

**A decision calendar built from lead time and option expiry.** The lead-time form of the last responsible moment ([Ballard and Zabelle 2000](http://p2sl.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/W010-Ballard_Zabelle-2000-Lean-Design-Process-Tools-and-Techniques-LCI-White-Paper-10.pdf)), option expiry ([Matts and Maassen 2007](https://www.infoq.com/articles/real-options-enhance-agility/)) and Zimmermann's guidance models with a phase attribute per issue ([Zimmermann 2011](https://www.infoq.com/articles/ieee-arch-decisions/)) together describe a dated list of decisions. No source publishes one. Kruchten's 2004 ontology of decision types and Tyree and Akerman's 2005 template are the obvious companions; neither was opened.

**Regulatory stacking for products shipping in 2027 and 2028.** The Cyber Resilience Act, the AI Act's high-risk obligations, FDA's cybersecurity expectations and the revised sector standards will apply together to some products. The CRA's operative text was read only through third-party reproductions and should be checked against the Official Journal ([Regulation (EU) 2024/2847](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2847/oj/eng)). The harmonised standards under the AI Act were not researched, and the edition 2 status of IEC 62304 rests on one vendor article.

**Iterative development under certification.** SafeScrum ([Hanssen, Stålhane and Myklebust 2018](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-319-99334-8)), AAMI TIR45:2023 ([ANSI listing](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/aami/aamitir452023)) and the text of EN 50716 say which lifecycle activities stay outside the sprints; only descriptions of them were read. Fitzgerald and colleagues' R-Scrum study (ICSE 2013) could not be opened. The one cost figure found, verification and validation at 75% of time, comes from a single railway case.

**Model-based gates and the defence rewrite.** The 2023 edition of 15288 adds an MBSE annex and INCOSE's Vision 2035 expects practice to be "largely model-based" ([INCOSE Vision 2035](https://www.incose.org/incose-se-vision-2035-executive-summary/)), yet no source defines what state a model must be in at each gate. GAO reports that four of 48 programmes were developing or planning a full system-level digital twin ([GAO-26-108457 report](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-108457.pdf)). Whether DoD's promised consolidation of milestones changes the PDR and CDR requirements is unknown as of October 2026.

**Tenant and data model as the founder's Type 1 decision.** The Slack and Notion accounts and the AWS SaaS Lens are consistent, but they are two case reports and vendor guidance. Golding's *Building Multi-Tenant SaaS Architectures* was seen as a table of contents only ([O'Reilly listing](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/building-multi-tenant-saas/9781098140632/)), and no rigorous study of the cost of migrating an authorisation or tenancy model was found.

**AI-native architecture.** DORA's 2025 amplifier finding, the 2015 warning on hidden technical debt in machine-learning systems and the 2024 practitioner essays agree that evaluation, data capture and delivery infrastructure are the durable decisions. No peer-reviewed study of architecture decisions in AI-native startups after 2020 was found, and Huyen's *AI Engineering* was seen as contents only ([O'Reilly listing](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/ai-engineering/9781098166298/)).

**Conway, mirroring and premature modularisation.** Colfer and Baldwin's review is the best single source on when mirroring helps and when it traps, and its ten "premature modularization" cases deserve a close reading ([Colfer and Baldwin 2016](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/16-124_7ae90679-0ce6-4d72-9e9d-828872c7af49.pdf)). The second edition of *Team Topologies* (September 2025) was not opened ([IT Revolution](https://itrevolution.com/product/team-topologies-second-edition/)). Evidence for the inverse Conway manoeuvre is absent from everything read.

**Unread primaries and unverified leads.** Several sources central to the question could not be opened, and what follows is recollection to be checked, not finding. Royce's 1970 paper is recalled as adding five steps to the plain sequence, beginning with "program design comes first" and including "do it twice"; only secondary accounts were read ([Wikipedia, Winston W. Royce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_W._Royce)). Maier's 2006 paper on reconciling system and software architecture (DOI 10.1002/sys.20050) is recalled as arguing that hardware-shaped decomposition and review timing conflict with layered software architecture. McGraw's estimate that about half of security defects are design flaws is recalled as experience-based, not measured. Also unopened: Boehm's 1996 "Anchoring the Software Process", the Waterman ICSE paper itself, Hatcliff and colleagues' 2014 survey of certification challenges, Leveson's *Engineering a Safer World*, and Honour's dissertation.

## Conclusion

Fifty years of process literature have changed the size of the thing committed to far more than the rule for committing. The invariant is evidence before escalation; what shrank is the unit, from a whole system at a design review to an increment behind a skeleton, a fitness function and a decision record. Seen that way, the regulated-domain and startup literatures describe the same class of decision in different words. Allocating an ASIL, an assurance level or a security level to a partition and choosing a tenant or partition key both assign a property to an architectural region that everything downstream inherits, and both are cheap on the day and very expensive after others have built on them. A founder's practical instrument is therefore a short, dated register of such decisions, each with its lead time, its expiry and the evidence that would justify it, kept separate from the much larger set that should be decided fast and locally.

The evidence does not license more confidence than that. The only quantified sizing rule predates the tooling that changed the cost of change, the best field data concern technology maturity and team structure and not software architecture as such, and the experiment that would settle the matter has not been run. For a reader who is both architect and researcher, that last gap is the opportunity: decision timing is now observable in version-controlled records, and no study found has yet used it to test the claim on which every canon rests.
