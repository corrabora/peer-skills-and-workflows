# Specs pay off as reviewed contracts with a checkable oracle and an accountable owner, not as bigger prompts

Research date: 2026-10-26. Status: independent study by the Fable peer in a two-peer research exercise; it informs a later joint recommendation and changes no workflow rule. "Spec-driven development" (SDD) is the 2025–2026 name for an old discipline: write down what the system must do, and why, before and while building it, and treat that text as the thing implementation answers to. The new element is the consumer. The spec is now read by a coding agent, and the clarification benchmarks show that such agents tend to proceed on ambiguous text rather than ask unless the process makes them. The current tools (Kiro, GitHub Spec Kit, OpenSpec, Tessl) converge on a specify → plan → tasks → implement sequence, usually with human checkpoints between stages; Kiro's Quick Spec and OpenSpec's three-step flow are the lighter variants. They do not agree on what a spec is, how big it should be, or whether it outlives the change. Vendor material offers no outcome measurements. The largest observational test, 88,052 pull requests in 119 open-source repositories, found no defect or rework benefit from specification artifacts once task complexity is controlled, with the caveat that those artifacts were mostly issue references rather than SDD tool output. The one controlled human pilot my search found (Garg 2026, in the evidence table) shows attribution rather than defect discovery as the measured benefit of a specification baseline, at a review cost, and a separate industrial experiment (Tufano et al. 2026) shows a gain from writing contracts before generating tests.

The older literature is where the usable detail lives. Four decades of requirements engineering, formal specification practice at Amazon, behaviour-driven development, and design-document cultures converge on five properties of a specification that earns its keep: it describes the world the system must change rather than the machine; it is small enough to be read and precise where errors are expensive; every statement has an oracle (an example, a test, a model, or a measurable threshold) that can tell whether it is met; its vocabulary is designated and its gaps are marked rather than papered over; and it records the decisions and rejected alternatives that explain it. Each property has a failure mode with a name and a counter-practice, and several have measured effects.

For software and systems plans, the adaptation is direct. A plan is a specification of work plus the design decisions it commits to. The practices that transfer are: choose the ceremony by uncertainty, impact, interfaces and reversibility so small changes skip it; elicit intent through a one-question-at-a-time interview; separate requirements, domain assumptions, and the implementable specification; write acceptance criteria in a constrained syntax with a named oracle each; run a requirements-quality pass before anyone plans against the text; record decisions as short, superseded-not-deleted records; review from assigned perspectives with frozen independent assessments; make the operator's gate a decision rather than a monitoring task; implement from the approved baseline with its constraints rather than from the drafting conversation; and detect and reconcile drift between plan and code at checkpoints, through deltas rather than rewrites.

## Scope, method and evidence labels

I read primary sources where they were reachable: the tool documentation and announcements, the practitioner evaluations, the standards material I could access, and the original papers on requirements, specification, formal methods, reading techniques, traceability, automation and developer productivity. Twenty-three papers were downloaded and read from locally extracted text; their SHA-256 hashes are in the source manifest. Where a primary source was unreachable (ACM paywall or 403, dead hosts) I say so and mark the claim as second-hand. I did not read the repository's plan-coordinator skill or related plan contracts, as the brief requires; the earlier research reports in `docs/research/` were inspected for format only. Verbatim quotation is kept to a few words per source; exact wording can be read at the cited location.

Labels used in the text:

- **Normative:** a standard or guide prescribes it (ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148, EARS, Gherkin, a process document such as Oxide's RFD 1).
- **Claim:** an author or vendor asserts it without a reported measurement.
- **Measured:** a study, case study or experiment reports a result; its design and limits are stated beside the number.
- **Adaptation:** my proposal for this repository's planning work, not something a source prescribes.

Access codes in the reference list: F = full text read, P = partially read, A = abstract or landing page only, S = read through a secondary source, N = not opened.

## The 2025 and 2026 tools agree on a pipeline but not on what a spec is

**Claim (definitions).** Böckeler's October 2025 evaluation on martinfowler.com, the only hands-on comparison of the three tools that labelled themselves SDD at the time, distinguishes three levels. *Spec-first* writes a considered spec and uses it for the task at hand. *Spec-anchored* keeps the spec after the task so it can serve later evolution and maintenance. *Spec-as-source* makes the spec the only artifact a human edits, with code regenerated from it. Every tool she examined is spec-first; only Tessl explicitly aims at spec-anchored and explores spec-as-source. She also observes that the term has already diffused, to the point where some people use "spec" to mean a detailed prompt ([Böckeler 2025](https://martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/sdd-3-tools.html)).

Thoughtworks placed the technique in *Assess* in November 2025, describing it as an emerging AI-assisted workflow that starts from a structured functional specification and decomposes it into solutions and tasks. The blip cautions that the workflows are elaborate and opinionated, that behaviour varies with task size, that some tools emit long spec files that are hard to review, and that the field may be relearning that hand-crafted detailed rules for AI do not scale ([Thoughtworks Radar, SDD, Nov 2025](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/techniques/spec-driven-development)). The April 2026 Radar names "semantic diffusion" of terms such as SDD and harness engineering as a theme, keeps Spec Kit and OpenSpec in Assess, and observes two camps forming: teams relying on improving agents with minimal structure, and teams preferring defined workflows and detailed specifications ([Thoughtworks Radar, Spec Kit, Apr 2026](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/languages-and-frameworks/github-spec-kit), [Thoughtworks Radar, OpenSpec, Apr 2026](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-us/radar/tools/openspec)). The Wikipedia article (revision of 29 September 2026) notes that the phrase predates the tools; it cites Ostroff, Makalsky and Paige's 2004 paper on agile specification-driven development ([Wikipedia, Spec-driven development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spec-driven_development)). The host study's analytical split is sharper than the tool vocabulary and is worth carrying alongside it: a spec may *guide* development, may *remain authoritative* through change, or may *generate or verify* software, and each claim needs different evidence (`spec-driven-development-codex.md`).

Two design choices stand out. Spec Kit's `clarify` command scans a draft against ten coverage categories (functional scope, domain and data model, interaction flow, non-functional attributes, integrations, edge cases and failure handling, constraints and trade-offs, terminology, completion signals, placeholders), caps a session at five questions, asks exactly one question per turn, and writes each accepted answer back into the spec under a dated clarifications heading ([Spec Kit clarify command](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/github/spec-kit/67e0f7294e6e728ab60bf7b4590deb470448bc2d/templates/commands/clarify.md)). Its `checklist` command treats a checklist as unit tests for the requirements text: items probe completeness, clarity, consistency, measurability and coverage of the English, and must not be implementation tests; the template rewrites an item like verifying that a page shows three cards into a question about whether the number and layout are specified ([Spec Kit checklist command](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/github/spec-kit/67e0f7294e6e728ab60bf7b4590deb470448bc2d/templates/commands/checklist.md)). Both are process answers to the problem the requirements literature names below: incomplete and ambiguous text that the reader, now an agent, will resolve silently.

**Claim (the practitioner lineage).** The tools formalise patterns practitioners had already published. Harper Reed's February 2025 workflow has a model interview him one question at a time until a developer-ready spec can be compiled into `spec.md`, has a reasoning model draft `prompt_plan.md` as small, iterative chunks that build on each other, and uses `todo.md` as a checklist the coding model ticks off; he warns that the process is largely single-player and that progress can outrun the author's understanding ([Reed 2025](https://harper.blog/2025/02/16/my-llm-codegen-workflow-atm/)). Anthropic's Claude Code guidance recommends having the model interview the user in detail, then write a complete spec to a file, then execute it in a fresh session with clean context; it describes the most useful specs as self-contained, naming files and interfaces, stating what is out of scope, and ending with an end-to-end verification step. It also offers a triage heuristic: when the diff can be described in one sentence, skip the plan ([Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). OpenAI's harness-engineering account treats the repository as the single system of record for product principles, engineering norms and decisions, keeps `AGENTS.md` to about a hundred lines as a table of contents pointing to design docs, product specs and execution plans, and has humans specify intent and turn user feedback into acceptance criteria while agents write the code; its claim of a tenfold time reduction is labelled an estimate ([Lopopolo 2026](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/), read via a mirror).

**Claim (the strong thesis).** Sean Grove's "The New Code" talk argues that code is a small fraction of an engineer's impact and structured communication the rest, that whoever writes the spec is now the programmer, and that OpenAI's Model Spec, a versioned set of Markdown files whose clauses carry identifiers linked to challenging prompts used as success criteria, is a spec that is executable because it trains and evaluates the model. He lists open questions: real situations are messy, applying principles consistently takes interpretive work, and people cannot reliably direct systems toward goals they have not articulated ([Grove 2025](https://ai.engineer/talks/8rABwKRsec4-specifications-are-the-new-code)). The Model Spec itself (version 2026-08-18) is organised as a chain of command with authority levels (Root, System, Developer, User, Guideline) and per-clause examples ([OpenAI Model Spec 2026-08-18](https://model-spec.openai.com/2026-08-18.html)). It is a useful existence proof that a natural-language spec can be a maintained, tested artifact, but it is a governance document for a product, not evidence about software delivery.

Three conclusions follow. First, the strongest measured benefits of specification come from making it *checkable*: model checking a design, conformance-testing against a reference model, or reading from assigned perspectives. Prose that no tool and no second reader can check has no measured benefit in this corpus beyond the ambiguity reductions of a constrained syntax. Second, the economic argument most often used to justify heavy up-front specification, the escalating cost of late fixes, is weaker than its reputation, and its own originators attached it to emergent requirements and prototyping rather than to complete prior specification. Third, in the agentic setting the measured benefits so far are specific and small: attribution in review and a preliminary, confounded generation gain (Garg row), and contract-first test generation (Tufano et al. row), at a measurable review cost, while the largest observational test finds no benefit from artifact presence alone (Hill row). That is a narrower claim than "specs are the new code," and it is the one a workflow should be built on.

## Five properties of a specification that earns its keep

The canon from the 1980s and 1990s was written for human readers, but its reasoning about text that will be acted on applies with more force when the reader is an agent that does not push back. Each property below names the sources, the failure it prevents, and the check that tests for it.

### 1. It describes the world, not the machine

**Normative.** Zave and Jackson's "Four Dark Corners" grounds every requirements statement in the environment the system will change rather than in the machine. They distinguish two moods: *indicative* statements describe the environment as it is regardless of the machine (domain knowledge, assumptions), and *optative* statements describe the environment as the customer wants it to become (requirements). A specification is an optative statement that is directly implementable, and the three are tied by a proof obligation: the specification S together with the domain knowledge K must entail the requirements R, and S and K must be consistent. Requirements that cannot be implemented directly are refined into specifications by appeal to domain knowledge; supporting that refinement is, in their account, what domain knowledge is for ([Zave and Jackson 1997](http://www.pamelazave.com/4dc.pdf), §3.2, §5, §6.1). Jackson's ICSE paper states the point in one sentence, "the problem is in the world; the machine is the solution we construct," and catalogues four ways developers deny the world: by prior knowledge, by hacking, by abstraction, and by vagueness, the last meaning descriptions of the machine passed off as descriptions of the world ([Jackson 1995](https://users.ece.utexas.edu/~perry/education/SE-Intro/jackson-icse95.pdf), §3).

**Normative.** Meyer's sin of *overspecification* is text that describes a feature of a possible solution rather than of the problem ([Meyer 1985](https://se.inf.ethz.ch/~meyer/publications/ieee/formalism.pdf), Table 1). Spec Kit's template makes the same rule in current vocabulary: the spec covers what users need and why, and leaves out stack, APIs and code structure ([Spec Kit, spec-driven.md](https://github.com/github/spec-kit/blob/67e0f7294e6e728ab60bf7b4590deb470448bc2d/spec-driven.md)). Gherkin guidance asks authors to write examples that assume no particular technology or interface and to assert only outcomes observable by the user or an external system ([Gherkin Reference](https://cucumber.io/docs/gherkin/reference/)).

**Why it matters more for agents.** Böckeler reports being repeatedly unsure, inside the tools, when to stay functional and when to add technical detail ([Böckeler 2025](https://martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/sdd-3-tools.html)). NaPiRE's seventh-ranked problem is stakeholders who cannot separate requirements from solution designs they already have in mind ([Méndez Fernández et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/html/1611.10288)). Agarwal et al.'s interviewees agree specs should capture intent yet often put implementation detail into them, and note that such detail makes specs decay ([Agarwal et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25202)). The Zave–Jackson split is the tool that removes the confusion: a requirement is a desired property of the world; an assumption is something the world will do without us; a specification is what the machine commits to at the shared interface. Writing the three in separate columns makes the "how" leak visible and, more usefully, makes the *assumptions* visible. In the Fonseca study and the AWS experience, the failures that mattered sat exactly at that boundary, in assumptions about the network, the file system, or the shim between verified and unverified code ([Fonseca et al. 2017](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/pfonseca/papers/eurosys2017-dsbugs.pdf), [Newcombe et al. 2015](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/formal-methods-amazon.pdf)).

**Check.** For each statement, classify it R, K or S. Any S that names an internal mechanism not observable at the interface is implementation bias unless the operator has decided it as a constraint. Any R with no K and S beneath it is not yet implementable.

### 2. It is small enough to review and precise where errors are expensive

**Claim and Measured.** AWS wanted to capture the essence of a design in a few hundred lines of precise text, because conventional design documents of prose, diagrams and ad hoc pseudo-code are ambiguous and omit partial failure and the granularity of concurrency, while executable code is unambiguous but drowns the reader in detail ([Newcombe et al. 2015](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/formal-methods-amazon.pdf)). The ShardStore reference model is about one percent of the implementation's size ([Bornholt et al. 2021](https://www.amazon.science/publications/using-lightweight-formal-methods-to-validate-a-key-value-storage-node-in-amazon-s3), slides). Nygard keeps decision records to one or two pages on the argument that large documents are never kept current and small modular ones have at least a chance ([Nygard 2011](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions)). Google design docs run ten to twenty pages for larger projects, with one-to-three-page mini-docs, and are warned against copying interface or schema definitions that fall out of date ([Ubl 2020](https://www.industrialempathy.com/posts/design-docs-at-google/)). Preston-Werner's README-first argument is that a single introductory file penalises long or over-precise specification, since large systems specified in minute detail tend to be the wrong systems specified in minute detail ([Preston-Werner 2010](https://tom.preston-werner.com/2010/08/23/readme-driven-development.html)). Basecamp's pitch deliberately stops at breadboards and thick-marker sketches so as to add concreteness without over-specifying the design ([Shape Up, ch. 6](https://basecamp.com/shapeup/1.5-chapter-06)).

**Measured (in the negative).** The 2025–2026 complaints are about size: sixteen acceptance criteria for a bug fix; overly verbose Markdown outputs that increased cognitive load; agent instruction bloat, where long, sometimes conflicting instruction files cause rules to be ignored and material in the middle of a long context gets less attention ([Böckeler 2025](https://martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/sdd-3-tools.html), [Thoughtworks Radar, Spec Kit, Apr 2026](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/languages-and-frameworks/github-spec-kit), [Thoughtworks Radar, Agent instruction bloat, Apr 2026](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/techniques/agent-instruction-bloat)). Anthropic's own guidance warns that a bloated instruction file leads the model to ignore the instructions that matter and suggests testing each line by asking whether removing it would cause mistakes ([Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)).

**Where precision pays.** Hillel Wayne separates design specification (intentions for the overall system) from code specification, notes that industrial formal-methods users mostly specify and verify designs, and argues that the main benefit of writing a design spec is being forced to understand what one is writing ([Wayne 2019](https://www.hillelwayne.com/post/why-dont-people-use-formal-methods/)). Brooker adds the limit: safety and liveness are a small part of the design picture; latency, cost, overload behaviour and sensitivity to network conditions need quantitative models and real data, and quantitative design forces crisp requirements ([Brooker 2022](https://brooker.co.za/blog/2022/06/02/formal.html)). Lamport's blueprint analogy makes the same proportionality argument: most code needs only an informal specification while concurrent and distributed designs need a formal one; specifications should not be written in program code and can live as comments beside it ([Lamport 2015](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2736348), read second-hand).

**Check.** The spec fits in one sitting. Each section earns its place by answering a question the implementer or reviewer would otherwise guess at. Precision (an example, a formula, a model) is spent where a wrong guess is expensive or hard to reverse, and nowhere else.

### 3. Every requirement has an oracle

**Normative.** ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018 clause 5.2.5 requires each requirement to be necessary, appropriate, unambiguous, complete, singular, feasible, verifiable, correct and conforming, and clause 5.2.6 requires each *set* to be complete, consistent, feasible, comprehensible and able to be validated ([ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018](https://www.iso.org/standard/72089.html), via the Extuitive summary; NASA's Systems Engineering Handbook Appendix C, read by the host study, is an inspected primary alternative). Meyer's *wishful thinking* sin is text that defines a feature so that no candidate solution could realistically be validated against it ([Meyer 1985](https://se.inf.ethz.ch/~meyer/publications/ieee/formalism.pdf)). North's BDD made the oracle the unit of specification: a scenario has givens, an event and expected outcomes, and he argues that requirements are behaviour too ([North 2006](https://dannorth.net/blog/introducing-bdd/)). Adzic's seven process patterns, distilled from about fifty projects (deriving scope from goals, specifying collaboratively, illustrating using examples, refining the specification, automating validation without changing specifications, validating frequently, evolving a documentation system), end in living documentation that teams credited for confidence when rebuilding systems ([Adzic 2011](https://gojko.net/books/specification-by-example/), chapter 2). Key examples, in his account, serve both as the development target and as the acceptance check.

**Normative (constrained syntax).** EARS reduces natural-language requirements to five templates: ubiquitous (`The <system> shall <response>`), event-driven (`WHEN <trigger> the <system> shall <response>`), unwanted behaviour (`IF <trigger>, THEN the <system> shall <response>`), state-driven (`WHILE <state> …`) and optional feature (`WHERE <feature> …`), with preconditions placed before the trigger so that a requirement fires only when its preconditions already hold ([Mavin et al. 2009](https://ccy05327.github.io/SDD/08-PDF/Easy%20Approach%20to%20Requirements%20Syntax%20(EARS).pdf), §4). Kiro adopted the event-driven form as its acceptance-criterion syntax; OpenSpec uses SHALL plus WHEN/THEN scenarios ([Kiro specs documentation](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/), [OpenSpec README](https://github.com/Fission-AI/OpenSpec/blob/6d04a1b58dedc95729d34e4f223d9d483aae210a/README.md)).

**Measured.** The oracle is what the measured successes have in common: TLA+ properties checked against a design, a reference model checked against an implementation, acceptance scenarios executed against code, clause-linked prompts graded against a model spec, contracts written before tests. Fonseca's two specification bugs were found by asking what the spec *failed* to forbid and by negative testing, mutating the implementation to disable deduplication and observing that it still verified ([Fonseca et al. 2017](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/pfonseca/papers/eurosys2017-dsbugs.pdf), §5). Clover generalises the idea for LLM output: check code, docstring and formal annotation for mutual consistency and reject anything inconsistent, achieving up to 87% acceptance of correct programs with no false acceptances on its benchmark ([Sun et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17807), second-hand). ClarifyGPT uses the same trick upstream: generate several candidate solutions and treat disagreement as a sign that the requirement is ambiguous ([Mu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10996)).

**Check.** Beside each requirement, name the oracle: an example with inputs and expected outcome, a test, a model property, a measured threshold, or an inspection with a stated observation, a pass/fail meaning and a judge. Phrases such as "works properly" are banned ([Spec Kit checklist command](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/github/spec-kit/67e0f7294e6e728ab60bf7b4590deb470448bc2d/templates/commands/checklist.md)). A requirement without an oracle is underspecified: give it one, or let the operator deliberately reclassify it as a goal.

### 4. Its vocabulary is designated and its gaps are marked

**Normative.** Zave and Jackson hold that the only way to fix the meaning of a primitive term is an informal, written, maintained explanation, which they call a designation; their student/course dialogue shows two engineers agreeing a formula while meaning different things by one word ([Zave and Jackson 1997](http://www.pamelazave.com/4dc.pdf), §2). Parnas and Clements list confusing and inconsistent terminology among the organisational causes of unusable documentation ([Parnas and Clements 1986](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/~nr/cs257/archive/david-parnas/fake-it.pdf), §VI.A), and Berry's handbook classifies the ambiguities that survive careful writing as lexical, syntactic, semantic and pragmatic, plus vagueness and language error ([Berry, Kamsties and Krieger 2003](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~dberry/handbook/ambiguityHandbook.pdf), §3.3).

**Normative (gaps).** Parnas and Clements' rule for missing information is to note the gap where the information belongs and proceed as if that part were expected to change ([Parnas and Clements 1986](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/~nr/cs257/archive/david-parnas/fake-it.pdf), §VII). Spec Kit's `[NEEDS CLARIFICATION: specific question]` marker is the same rule, introduced so that the model flags ambiguity instead of guessing ([Spec Kit, spec-driven.md](https://github.com/github/spec-kit/blob/67e0f7294e6e728ab60bf7b4590deb470448bc2d/spec-driven.md)). Meyer's *silence* (a feature of the problem the text never covers) and *forward reference* (a term used before it is defined) are the two sins this property addresses ([Meyer 1985](https://se.inf.ethz.ch/~meyer/publications/ieee/formalism.pdf)).

**Measured.** NaPiRE's two most-cited problems, incomplete or hidden requirements and customer communication flaws, are gaps and vocabulary failures ([Méndez Fernández et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/html/1611.10288)). ClarifyCodeBench's finding that clarification quality falls sharply as ambiguity density rises says the agent will not find the gaps for you once there are several ([Fang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00711)).

**Check.** A glossary of designated terms travels with the spec. Every consequential term in a requirement, one whose reading would change a test or a responsibility, appears in it or in the project glossary. Every unknown is a marked question with an owner, never an assumption written as a fact.

### 5. It records decisions and the alternatives it rejected

**Normative.** Parnas and Clements treat documentation as the medium of design: no decision counts until it is in the documents, and they depart from their own ideal in one respect, by recording every alternative considered and rejected together with the reason, so that later readers, including maintainers years on, can find out why ([Parnas and Clements 1986](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/~nr/cs257/archive/david-parnas/fake-it.pdf), §VII). Nygard's decision record has context (the forces at play, stated neutrally), decision (in the active voice), status (proposed, accepted, deprecated, superseded) and consequences, all of them and not only the positive ones; reversed decisions are kept and marked superseded ([Nygard 2011](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions)). Google's design doc template includes goals and non-goals, alternatives considered, and cross-cutting concerns, and Ubl names organisational memory of design decisions among the document's purposes ([Ubl 2020](https://www.industrialempathy.com/posts/design-docs-at-google/)). Basecamp's pitch names *rabbit holes* and *no-gos* explicitly ([Shape Up, ch. 6](https://basecamp.com/shapeup/1.5-chapter-06)).

**Measured.** Gotel and Finkelstein's study of more than 100 practitioners found that most problems attributed to poor requirements traceability came from inadequate *pre-specification* traceability: the record of where a requirement came from, who decided it and why, as opposed to post-specification links from requirement to code ([Gotel and Finkelstein 1994](http://csis.pace.edu/~ogotel/research/GOTEL94%20An%20Analysis%20of%20the%20Requirements%20Traceability%20Problem.pdf), §5.4). Tooling then and now supplies the post-specification links; the pre-specification record is what a decision log supplies.

**Check.** Each consequential choice in the spec points to a decision record with alternatives and the reason. Each `[NEEDS CLARIFICATION]` that is resolved becomes either a designation, an assumption (K), or a decision record.

## Validation checks the text before anyone plans against it

The sources describe four layers of validation. They are cheap in the order listed and should run in that order.

**Layer 1: a quality pass against named defect classes (Normative).** Meyer's seven sins are a checklist a reader can apply in minutes; ISO 29148's nine characteristics are the standard's version of the same list. Requirement smells (subjective language, ambiguous adverbs and adjectives, loopholes, open-ended non-verifiable terms, superlatives, comparatives, negative statements, vague pronouns, incomplete references) can be detected automatically at 59% precision and 82% recall ([Femmer et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.08847)); that is a useful pre-filter, not a verdict. Spec Kit's checklist command turns the pass into generated questions about the English ([Spec Kit checklist command](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/github/spec-kit/67e0f7294e6e728ab60bf7b4590deb470448bc2d/templates/commands/checklist.md)).

| Meyer's sin (1985) | Meaning, paraphrased from Table 1 | ISO 29148 characteristic violated |
|---|---|---|
| Noise | Text that tells the reader nothing about the problem, including repetition and second thoughts | Necessary |
| Silence | An aspect of the problem the text never addresses | Complete |
| Overspecification | Text about a possible solution rather than about the problem | Appropriate |
| Contradiction | Two passages that fix the same feature in incompatible ways | Consistent (set) |
| Ambiguity | Text that a competent reader can take in at least two ways | Unambiguous |
| Forward reference | A term used before the text defines it | Comprehensible (set) |
| Wishful thinking | A feature stated so that no solution could realistically be checked against it | Verifiable |

**Layer 2: a clarification interview (Normative and Measured).** The gaps the quality pass marks are closed by asking the person who owns the answer. The current consensus is one question at a time: Reed's interview prompt, Spec Kit's one-question rule with a five-question session cap, Anthropic's detailed-interview prompt ([Reed 2025](https://harper.blog/2025/02/16/my-llm-codegen-workflow-atm/), [Spec Kit clarify command](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/github/spec-kit/67e0f7294e6e728ab60bf7b4590deb470448bc2d/templates/commands/clarify.md), [Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). The cap is a usability choice, not an adequacy test. The interview needs a subject-matter boundary, for the reason given under property 4: designated phenomena bound the space of alternative ways to meet a goal. ClarifyGPT shows that asking raises pass rates by eight to ten points; ClarifyCodeBench shows models will not ask well without being made to ([Mu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10996), [Fang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00711)).

**Layer 3: review from assigned perspectives (Measured).** Perspective-based reading assigns each reviewer a role (tester, developer, user) and an operational scenario: the tester drafts test cases from the document, the developer drafts a high-level design, the user drafts a user manual; simulated teams composed from independent readers covered significantly more defects than the organisation's usual technique, while individual-level results were mixed ([Basili et al. 1996](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~mvz/handouts/emp_pbr.pdf)). The adaptation to test in agent peer review of a spec is to give each reviewer one perspective and a concrete artifact to derive, and to count as defects what they could not derive. Boehm and Basili's summary figures (peer reviews catch about 60%; perspective-based reviews 35% more than undirected) come from human inspections and are not expected rates for agent review; they say only that review is necessary and insufficient ([Boehm and Basili 2001](https://www.cs.umd.edu/projects/SoftEng/ESEG/papers/82.78.pdf)). The host study adds a complementary review move from goal-oriented requirements engineering: for each critical outcome, construct a sequence in which every written rule holds and the outcome still fails (van Lamsweerde and Letier's obstacle analysis, read by the host).

**Layer 4: testing the spec itself (Measured).** For the parts of a spec that are precise enough, the spec can be executed against something. Three techniques appear in the sources: model-check a design against stated safety and liveness properties and explicit environmental assumptions, as AWS does (evidence table above); conformance-test an implementation against an executable reference model ([Bornholt et al. 2021](https://www.amazon.science/publications/using-lightweight-formal-methods-to-validate-a-key-value-storage-node-in-amazon-s3)); and negative-test the spec by deliberately breaking the implementation and confirming the spec notices ([Fonseca et al. 2017](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/pfonseca/papers/eurosys2017-dsbugs.pdf)). For natural-language acceptance criteria, the analogue is to write the example that would fail if the criterion were violated, and to check that the example is not already satisfied by a trivially wrong implementation.

**Refinement is learning, not completion (Normative and Claim).** Brooks calls deciding precisely what to build the hardest single part of building software, holds that clients cannot specify a modern product completely and correctly before trying versions of it, and recommends iterative requirements refinement with prototypes ([Brooks 1986](https://worrydream.com/refs/Brooks_1986_-_No_Silver_Bullet.pdf)). Parnas and Clements explain why no real project follows a rational process (requirements are unknown or change for external reasons, people err, prior designs intrude) and prescribe producing the documents anyway, in the ideal order, correcting them as errors appear ([Parnas and Clements 1986](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/~nr/cs257/archive/david-parnas/fake-it.pdf), §II, §VII). Kent Beck's January 2026 objection to SDD is the same point from the other side: the descriptions he has seen write the whole specification first and so assume nothing learned during implementation would change it, which he finds bizarre ([Fowler 2026](https://martinfowler.com/fragments/2026-01-08.html)). Boehm and Basili warn against counting cost-effective changes to a system's definition as avoidable defects ([Boehm and Basili 2001](https://www.cs.umd.edu/projects/SoftEng/ESEG/papers/82.78.pdf)). The workable position, which all of these authors share, is Parnas's: the spec is the medium in which learning is recorded, so it changes during implementation, and the discipline is that no decision counts until it is in the document.

## Evolution works through small, attributable deltas, not regeneration

**Normative (process cultures).** Oxide's RFD 1 gives each document a state: prediscussion, ideation, discussion, published, committed, abandoned. Discussion happens in a pull request; a published document can still be corrected, and each logically independent change gets its own branch and pull request with the original authors as reviewers ([Oxide RFD 1](https://rfd.shared.oxide.computer/rfd/0001)). Rust's RFC process holds a ten-day final comment period and insists that acceptance is not a rubber stamp and does not guarantee the feature will land ([Rust RFC process](https://github.com/rust-lang/rfcs/blob/master/README.md)). Google design docs drift from reality over time, and updates often accumulate into something like a constitution with amendments ([Ubl 2020](https://www.industrialempathy.com/posts/design-docs-at-google/)). Nygard supersedes rather than deletes ([Nygard 2011](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions)). The common shape: a document has a lifecycle state, changes are small and reviewed by the people who wrote the original, and history is preserved.

**Claim (the tools).** Kiro's best-practices page frames specs as objects of continuous refinement: update requirements, let the tool revise the design, then synchronise the task list; store specs beside the code they describe ([Kiro specs documentation](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/)). OpenSpec's distinctive choice is the delta: a change carries an added-requirements section, and archiving merges the delta into the live spec; Thoughtworks singles out this focus on deltas over complete up-front specifications as what suits existing systems ([OpenSpec README](https://github.com/Fission-AI/OpenSpec/blob/6d04a1b58dedc95729d34e4f223d9d483aae210a/README.md), [Thoughtworks Radar, OpenSpec, Apr 2026](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-us/radar/tools/openspec)). Garg's governance model (evidence table above) ends in a drift log, each divergence paired with the clause it violates, and a reconciliation record owned by a human. The Spec Growth Engine proposal adds a drift gate that blocks a merge when spec and code diverge, without reported results ([Grabowski 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27045)). Agarwal et al.'s interviewees check spec against code when a change is completed rather than continuously ([Agarwal et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25202)).

**Claim versus history (spec-as-source).** Tessl's bet is that specs rather than code are the primary artifact, generated code is marked do-not-edit, and a registry of more than 10,000 library specs keeps agents from hallucinating APIs ([Maple 2025](https://tessl.io/blog/tessl-launches-spec-driven-framework-and-registry/), [Böckeler 2025](https://martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/sdd-3-tools.html)). Böckeler suspects this combines the downsides of model-driven development and of LLMs, inflexibility and non-determinism, and observed different code from the same spec across runs. Panda's measurement is the same tension in numbers: the discipline that makes output verifiable (citations) makes it less deterministic ([Panda 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30689)). The MDE record is the relevant prior: generation of whole systems was rare, success came in narrow domains, and gains were eaten by training and organisational change ([Hutchinson, Whittle and Rouncefield 2014](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/69765/1/SO_SW_2012_12_0188.R1_Whittle.pdf)). The lesson is not that regeneration never works; it is that a plan should not bet on it, and that spec-anchored (keep and maintain the spec; let code be edited) is the level with historical support.

**Claim (documentation as system of record).** OpenAI's harness account keeps everything an agent needs discoverable in the repository, on the principle that knowledge outside the repository does not exist for the agent, and enforces architectural rules mechanically with linters whose messages include remediation steps rather than relying on documentation alone ([Lopopolo 2026](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/), second-hand). Thoughtworks' caution about instruction bloat recommends progressive disclosure that surfaces only what the agent needs for its current task ([Thoughtworks Radar, Agent instruction bloat, Apr 2026](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/techniques/agent-instruction-bloat)). Both point the same way as Parnas and Clements' complaint about documentation organised by the order of discovery (property 4): structure the record so that a reader, human or agent, can find the part that governs the current task, and keep the entry point short.

**Adaptation.** Treat the spec as spec-anchored. Changes to it are deltas with their own review by the original authors or their successors. Each implementation checkpoint produces a drift log (code behaviour not in the spec; spec clauses not in the code; assumptions found false) and a reconciliation owned by a human where the drift changes accepted behaviour or assumptions: change the code, change the spec, or record an accepted deviation. Repairs that restore already accepted behaviour are applied within existing authority and logged. Supersede, never delete.

## The human belongs at decisions, not at a monitoring station

**Measured (automation research).** Bainbridge's 1983 paper identifies the ironies that still describe agentic coding. A designer who regards the human operator as unreliable and tries to eliminate them commits two ironies: the designer's own errors become a major source of operating problems, and the operator is left with exactly the tasks the designer could not automate. An operator reduced to monitoring loses manual and cognitive skill while being asked to intervene in the hardest cases, so the job needs more skill rather than less, and the vigilance literature shows humans cannot sustain attention for rare events ([Bainbridge 1983](https://ckrybus.com/static/papers/Bainbridge_1983_Automatica.pdf), §1). Parasuraman and Riley's review adds *misuse* (over-reliance that produces monitoring failures and decision biases) and *disuse* (neglect caused by false alarms) ([Parasuraman and Riley 1997](https://doi.org/10.1518/001872097778543886), abstract only).

**Measured (2025–2026).** METR's participants believed AI had sped them up by 20% while measurement showed a 19% slowdown; the organisation's 2026 data could not estimate the effect reliably because participants held back tasks they did not want to do without AI ([METR 2025](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/), [METR 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)). Garg's pilot (evidence table) shows the same gap between felt and measured benefit on the review side. Böckeler names the resulting risk a false sense of control: a detailed spec the agent then did not honour ([Böckeler 2025](https://martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/sdd-3-tools.html)). DORA's amplifiers include working in small batches and strong version control, and AI adoption still correlates negatively with stability ([DORA 2025 report](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report), [DORA AI Capabilities Model 2025](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/2025_dora_ai_capabilities_model.pdf)).

**What the sources say humans must supply.** Reading these together with the tool designs gives a short list of things an agent cannot be allowed to resolve by default:

- **Goals, appetite and non-goals.** Goals regress without a stated subject matter (property 1); Shape Up's appetite (the time the team wants to spend, as distinct from an estimate) and no-gos ([Shape Up, ch. 6](https://basecamp.com/shapeup/1.5-chapter-06)).
- **Designations.** What the consequential words mean in this project's world (property 4).
- **Domain assumptions (K).** What the environment is assumed to do, stated explicitly as the AWS practice does (property 1).
- **Trade-offs and accepted risks.** Decision records with alternatives (property 5).
- **Acceptance of the baseline and of each reconciliation that changes accepted behaviour.** In Garg's governance model the agent is responsible for generation but never accountable, and the approved baseline carries approver and timestamp.
- **Answers to the clarification questions.** ClarifyCodeBench shows agents do not ask well on their own ([Fang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00711)).

**What the gate should look like.** Our inference, not a compared design: Bainbridge's analysis argues against gates that ask a human to read a long generated document and approve it, since that is a monitoring task with poor vigilance and a perception gap behind it, and the better gate presents *decisions*: here are the open questions, here are the alternatives with consequences, here is the drift found between plan and code and the two ways to reconcile it, with links into the current spec so the whole artifact stays available. Kiro's per-phase review, Spec Kit's one-question-at-a-time clarification, and Oxide's hole-poking discussion all have this shape ([Kiro specs documentation](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/), [Spec Kit clarify command](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/github/spec-kit/67e0f7294e6e728ab60bf7b4590deb470448bc2d/templates/commands/clarify.md), [Oxide RFD 1](https://rfd.shared.oxide.computer/rfd/0001)). Anthropic's guidance that repeated corrections in one session signal cluttered context, and that a reviewer asked to find gaps will usually report some even when the work is sound, are operational warnings about the same monitoring failure from the other side ([Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)).

## Adapting the findings to software and systems plans

A plan is a specification of work. It states what will be true when the work is done (requirements), what it relies on (assumptions about the codebase, environment and people), what the worker commits to deliver (the implementable specification), the decisions it has already made and the ones it leaves open, and the order and checks by which the work proceeds. The mapping below is my adaptation; no source states it in these terms.

| Specification concept | Plan element | Source of the practice |
|---|---|---|
| Requirements (R, optative) | Outcome statements: observable properties of the system or repository when the plan is complete | [Zave and Jackson 1997](http://www.pamelazave.com/4dc.pdf) |
| Domain knowledge (K, indicative) | Assumptions: current behaviour, interfaces, environment facts, team constraints the plan relies on | [Zave and Jackson 1997](http://www.pamelazave.com/4dc.pdf), [Newcombe et al. 2015](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/formal-methods-amazon.pdf) |
| Specification (S) | Deliverables at the interface: files, APIs, behaviours, tests the worker will produce | [Zave and Jackson 1997](http://www.pamelazave.com/4dc.pdf) |

### Practices, each with its artifact and check

1. **Choose the ceremony before anything else.** Three levels, chosen by the operator or by stated criteria (uncertainty, impact, interfaces touched, reversibility), with the level and its reason recorded: *direct* (routine edits that need no spec; the skill is not invoked), *light* (a short plan: outcomes, assumptions, criteria with checks, tasks), *full* (the practices below). No page, sentence or question quota is an adequacy rule. Evidence: the ceremony complaints ([Böckeler 2025](https://martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/sdd-3-tools.html), [Thoughtworks Radar, Spec Kit, Apr 2026](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/languages-and-frameworks/github-spec-kit)), Kiro's Quick Spec, Garg's wasted cases, Agarwal et al.'s selective use, and Anthropic's one-sentence heuristic ([Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)).
2. **Elicit intent through an interview.** One consequential question at a time, or a small coherent batch when the operator prefers, after consulting accessible sources and settled decisions, each question tied to a coverage category (scope, data, interaction, non-functional, integration, failure handling, constraints, terminology, completion signal). Record each answer verbatim with date and answerer under a clarifications heading. Ask the owner of the answer; do not let the drafter answer its own question. No fixed cap; the operator may set one. Evidence: [Spec Kit clarify command](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/github/spec-kit/67e0f7294e6e728ab60bf7b4590deb470448bc2d/templates/commands/clarify.md), [Reed 2025](https://harper.blog/2025/02/16/my-llm-codegen-workflow-atm/), [Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices), [Mu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10996), [Fang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00711).
3. **Separate R, K and S.** A three-column table. Every K is a relied-upon assumption or future condition, recorded with its evidence, uncertainty, owner and consequence if false; every S is observable at the interface; every R has at least one K or S beneath it. Evidence: [Zave and Jackson 1997](http://www.pamelazave.com/4dc.pdf), [Jackson 1995](https://users.ece.utexas.edu/~perry/education/SE-Intro/jackson-icse95.pdf).
4. **Write criteria in a constrained syntax with an oracle each.** EARS for system behaviour, Given/When/Then for scenarios, a measured threshold for quality attributes. Name the check beside each. Evidence: [Mavin et al. 2009](https://ccy05327.github.io/SDD/08-PDF/Easy%20Approach%20to%20Requirements%20Syntax%20(EARS).pdf), [North 2006](https://dannorth.net/blog/introducing-bdd/), [ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018](https://www.iso.org/standard/72089.html).
5. **Run the quality pass before planning.** Meyer's seven sins and the smell list, by a reader who did not write the text; automated smell detection as a pre-filter. Evidence: [Meyer 1985](https://se.inf.ethz.ch/~meyer/publications/ieee/formalism.pdf), [Femmer et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.08847), [Spec Kit checklist command](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/github/spec-kit/67e0f7294e6e728ab60bf7b4590deb470448bc2d/templates/commands/checklist.md).
6. **Record decisions as short records.** Concise records sized to the decision: context, decision, alternatives rejected and why, consequences, status. Supersede rather than delete. Evidence: [Parnas and Clements 1986](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/~nr/cs257/archive/david-parnas/fake-it.pdf), [Nygard 2011](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions), [Gotel and Finkelstein 1994](http://csis.pace.edu/~ogotel/research/GOTEL94%20An%20Analysis%20of%20the%20Requirements%20Traceability%20Problem.pdf).
7. **Discover independently, draft once, review by reconstruction.** For a consequential slice, both peers first list rules, boundary examples, assumptions, obstacles, alternatives and open questions from the same brief, freeze and exchange; one owner then writes the single canonical draft and records which candidate elements were adopted. For review, give each reviewer one perspective and one artifact to derive: the implementer derives the task list and interfaces; the tester derives the checks; the operator derives the runbook or user path. A defect is anything they could not derive or derived differently. Freeze assessments before exchange; a coauthor's reread is recorded as such, and a fresh companion is required to claim an independent review. Evidence: Adzic's collaborative specification and Wynne's Example Mapping (read by the host study), [Basili et al. 1996](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~mvz/handouts/emp_pbr.pdf), [Boehm and Basili 2001](https://www.cs.umd.edu/projects/SoftEng/ESEG/papers/82.78.pdf).
8. **Make the operator's gate a decision list.** The gate presents open questions, decisions with alternatives and consequences, and the review's unresolved findings, each linked to the affected clauses, with the whole current spec available. It does not ask the operator to read and approve a long document. The approved baseline records approver and timestamp. Evidence: [Bainbridge 1983](https://ckrybus.com/static/papers/Bainbridge_1983_Automatica.pdf), [Garg 2026](https://www.infoq.com/articles/when-spec-driven-development-pays-off/), [Kiro specs documentation](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/).
9. **Implement from the approved baseline as the governing artifact.** The implementer receives the approved plan with its applicable constraints and evidence pointers, not the drafting conversation; starting in a fresh context is recommended for complex work and treated as a pilot variable rather than a rule. Evidence: Garg's staged arm against the single-prompt arm; Anthropic's fresh-session advice ([Garg 2026](https://www.infoq.com/articles/when-spec-driven-development-pays-off/), [Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)).
10. **Trace and check at checkpoints.** Each task carries its requirement identifiers. At each checkpoint, produce a drift log: behaviours in the code not in the plan, criteria in the plan not met, assumptions found false. Entries that change accepted behaviour or assumptions get a human reconciliation decision: change code, change plan (as a delta), or accept the deviation with a reason; repairs that restore accepted behaviour are applied within existing authority and logged. Work packages carry purpose, prerequisites, resources and interfaces, output, responsibility, linked requirement or risk, evidence method and environment, and exit decision, including investigative packages whose output is a measurement or a decision (NASA technical-planning guidance, read by the host study). Evidence: [Garg 2026](https://www.infoq.com/articles/when-spec-driven-development-pays-off/), [Kiro specs documentation](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/), [OpenSpec README](https://github.com/Fission-AI/OpenSpec/blob/6d04a1b58dedc95729d34e4f223d9d483aae210a/README.md), [Fonseca et al. 2017](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/pfonseca/papers/eurosys2017-dsbugs.pdf).
11. **Keep the plan alive by deltas and keep the entry point short.** The current plan is the baseline plus accepted deltas; the history is kept. An index or entry document points to the governing plan, decisions and glossary rather than repeating them. Evidence: [Oxide RFD 1](https://rfd.shared.oxide.computer/rfd/0001), [Nygard 2011](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions), [Lopopolo 2026](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/), [Thoughtworks Radar, Agent instruction bloat, Apr 2026](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/techniques/agent-instruction-bloat).
12. **Measure the practice.** Record, per plan: level chosen, interview questions asked, defects found at review by class, review time, drift entries by type, and rework after approval. Garg's attribution and time figures are the template; the point is to learn locally when the ceremony pays. Evidence: [Garg 2026](https://www.infoq.com/articles/when-spec-driven-development-pays-off/), [Hutchinson, Whittle and Rouncefield 2014](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/69765/1/SO_SW_2012_12_0188.R1_Whittle.pdf).

### Levels of ceremony

| Level | Use when | Artifacts | Gates |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct | Routine edit: low uncertainty and impact, no new interface, easily reversed | None beyond the commit message; the skill is not invoked | None |
| Light | Single component; clear requirements; low blast radius | Short plan: outcomes, assumptions, criteria with checks, tasks; glossary pointers | Operator answers marked questions; coauthor check; fresh review when the reviewer or operator asks for it |
| Full | Multi-constraint; new interfaces or data; regulated, long-lived or high-stakes; a capable-but-imperfect worker | R/K/S table, criteria with oracles, decision records, clarifications log, perspective review record, approved baseline, drift log | Clarification; independent discovery; fresh reconstruction review; operator decision gate; reconciliation at each checkpoint |

The evidence table's Garg and Agarwal et al. rows support reserving *full* for hard, multi-constraint work and for domains needing audit trails, and skipping it for throwaway scripts and tasks the assistant reliably completes in one pass.

## An illustrative application: idempotent webhook delivery

The example is invented to show the artifacts at the *full* level; it is not from a source. An operator asks for "reliable webhook delivery for order events."

**Triage.** New external interface, retries and persistence, customer-visible failure modes: *full*.

**Interview (three questions).**

- Q1: "Which observable property defines 'reliable' here: every event eventually delivered, no duplicate processing at the receiver, or both, and over what horizon?" → A: "Both, within 24 hours; after that we give up and alert."
- Q2: "Do receivers already de-duplicate, or must we make duplicates harmless?" → A: "Some receivers will not; make duplicates detectable with a stable key."
- Q3: "Appetite?" → A: "Two weeks; no new infrastructure beyond the existing queue."

**Designations.** *Order event*: a record created when an order changes state, identified by `event_id`. *Endpoint*: a customer-registered HTTPS URL. *Delivery attempt*: one HTTP POST of one event to one endpoint. *Delivered*: the endpoint returned 2xx within the timeout. *Idempotency key*: a value equal for every attempt of the same event to the same endpoint.

**R / K / S.**

| R (desired of the world) | K (assumed of the world) | S (what the service commits to) |
|---|---|---|
| R1 Every order event is delivered to each registered endpoint within 24 h, or an operator is alerted | K1 Endpoints are reachable most of the time; outages are minutes to hours, not days | S1 WHEN an order event is created THE SERVICE SHALL enqueue one delivery per registered endpoint within 5 s |
| R2 A receiver can detect duplicate deliveries of one event | K2 The existing queue delivers at-least-once; it may duplicate | S2 Every attempt SHALL carry header `Idempotency-Key = event_id` and identical body |
| R3 A failing endpoint does not delay other endpoints | K3 Receivers answer 2xx within 10 s when healthy | S3 IF an attempt fails or times out, THEN THE SERVICE SHALL retry that delivery with exponential backoff starting at 30 s, capped at 1 h, until 24 h from event creation |
| | | S4 WHEN 24 h elapse without delivery THE SERVICE SHALL mark the delivery failed and emit alert `webhook.delivery.failed` |
| | | S5 Deliveries to different endpoints SHALL be scheduled independently, and no single endpoint SHALL occupy more than a bounded share of delivery workers (proposed 25%) |

Candidate argument S, K ⊢ R, with gaps to investigate: R1 follows from S1, S3 and S4 given K1 and K3 only if queued deliveries survive a service restart, which no clause yet states; R2 follows from S2 given K2; R3 follows from S5 only if the worker bound holds under load, which needs a capacity check. The 24 h horizon was accepted in Q1; the 5 s and 10 s values, the backoff schedule and the 25% worker bound are proposals until the operator accepts them.

**Oracles.** S1: integration test creates an event, asserts two queue messages within 5 s. S2: contract test compares header and body hash across two attempts. S3: unit test with a fake clock asserts the backoff sequence 30 s, 60 s, …, 1 h, 1 h until 24 h; this shows the schedule, not that deliveries persist across a restart, which needs a separate crash-and-restart test. S4: test advances the clock 24 h and asserts the alert is emitted to a recorded sink; delivery through the real alert path needs an integration check in the target environment. S5: test stalls one endpoint and asserts the other's attempts proceed on schedule.

**Quality pass.** The first draft of S3 read "retry a reasonable number of times": *wishful thinking*. The interview answer fixed the horizon, so the clause now states it. The draft used "message" and "event" interchangeably: *ambiguity*; designations now fix one term. Nothing in S names the queue's implementation: no overspecification. Gap marked: `NEEDS CLARIFICATION: should a 410 Gone response stop retries early?` Owner: operator, before baseline approval.

**Decision record D1, retry policy.** Context: K1 and the 24 h horizon. Decision: exponential backoff with a 1 h cap. Alternatives rejected: fixed 5-minute interval (288 attempts per day per failing endpoint; wasteful under K1); unbounded exponential (would exceed the horizon with few attempts). Consequence: a receiver down for 23 h sees about 30 attempts. Status: proposed until the gate.

**Perspective review.** Implementer derives tasks T1–T4 and finds that S5 needs per-endpoint ordering to be stated or disclaimed; recorded as a finding. Tester derives the oracle list and finds no check for "identical body" when the event is later edited; finding. Operator perspective derives the runbook and asks what the alert payload must contain to act on it; finding. The host and companion freeze their assessments, exchange, and agree all three; the ordering question becomes clarification Q4.

**Gate.** The operator receives: Q4 (ordering), the 410 question, D1 for acceptance, the three findings with proposed resolutions, the proposed values, and the two gaps in the candidate argument (restart persistence; the worker bound under load). They decide: ordering is not required (recorded as a non-goal); 410 stops retries (new S6); D1 accepted; the 5 s, 10 s and 25% values accepted as initial targets. Baseline v1 is recorded with approver, timestamp and two named conditions: a restart-persistence clause and test (new S7) before any delivery code is merged, and a capacity check of the worker bound before S5 counts as met. Acceptance of the baseline authorises no implementation beyond the task already assigned.

**Implementation and drift.** A fresh worker receives the baseline. At checkpoint 1 it reports S7 and its crash-and-restart test as done, discharging the first condition, and that the library's backoff adds jitter of up to 10%, not in S3. Drift log entry: behaviour not in spec. Reconciliation (operator): accept, and amend S3 by delta to "approximately" with the jitter bound stated; the test's assertion is loosened to a range. At checkpoint 2 the worker finds no alert sink exists in the test environment: a missing assumption. Reconciliation: a test-fixture task for a fake sink, and a separate operator decision on provisioning or validating the real alert path, since a stub does not satisfy S4 in production. The archive keeps baseline v1 with its conditions, deltas Δ1 and Δ2, D1, the clarification log, and both review assessments.

Every clause names its check; every change has an owner; the candidate argument's gaps stay visible until evidence closes them.

## What remains unknown and how to refresh this study

**Open questions the sources do not settle.**

- Whether agent peer review of specs finds defects at the rates perspective-based reading found with humans. No study measures it; Garg's LLM reviewers (evidence table) showed the same pattern at lower recall.
- Whether the staged-generation gain survives controls for the extra model pass. Garg flags the confound; Rosa et al.'s registered report may answer part of it when Stage 2 publishes.
- Whether a question budget helps or hurts. Five is a design choice in Spec Kit, not a measured optimum, and the joint recommendation drops a fixed cap.
- Whether EARS-style criteria help agents as they helped human readers. Kiro adopted them; no published comparison exists.
- How much the review-time cost seen in Garg's pilot falls with practice or tooling.

**Refresh protocol.** Re-check, in this order: the Thoughtworks Radar technique and tool blips (SDD, OpenSpec, Spec Kit, agent instruction bloat) at each edition; the Spec Kit `templates/commands/` directory and `spec-driven.md` at the pinned commit of the day; Kiro's specs documentation; OpenSpec's README; arXiv and the ESEM, ASE and SANER proceedings for the Rosa et al. Stage 2 report and for follow-ups citing Hill, Garg, Tufano, Panda, Agarwal and ClarifyCodeBench; METR and DORA annual publications. Search strings that worked: `"spec-driven development" empirical`, `specification-driven code generation LLM study`, `requirements clarification LLM benchmark`. Sources not opened (Royce 1970, Leveson 2000, Lamport 2015 in full, Brooker and Desai 2025, Parasuraman and Riley 1997, Wirth 1971 in full) should be opened before any claim attributed to them is relied on beyond what is marked second-hand here.

## Conclusion

Spec-driven development, as the tools present it, is a specify → plan → tasks → implement sequence with human checkpoints in most variants and no outcome data. The older literature says what the checkpoints should check and why: whether the text is about the world rather than the machine, whether it is small and precise in the right places, whether each statement has an oracle, whether its words are designated and its gaps marked, and whether its decisions and rejected alternatives are recorded. The measured benefits in the agentic setting are specific and small (evidence table), and they come at a review cost that argues for choosing ceremony by risk. Our inference from the human-factors and perception-gap evidence, not a compared design, is that the operator's gate should present decisions rather than documents to approve. For planning work, the adaptation is a spec-anchored plan with a clarification interview, an R/K/S split, criteria with oracles, decision records, independent discovery followed by one canonical draft and reconstruction review, a decision-shaped operator gate, implementation from the approved baseline with its constraints, and drift reconciliation by delta at every checkpoint.
