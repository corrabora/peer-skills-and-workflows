# Peer decision making

Develop a recommendation the operator can accept, challenge or refine. The
calling agent is the **host**; one fresh, read-only worker is its **companion**.
They reason independently and then seek agreement as equals. A companion given
this skill participates without launching another peer.

## Use the peer protocol

Before preparing the companion's brief, both roles read
[peer-review](../peer-review/SKILL.md). Reuse its instructions for required
writing skills, Orca setup, engine/model/effort selection, shared source
snapshots, companion permissions, independent frozen assessments, paired
discussion rounds, explicit agreement, retained records and worker cleanup.
That skill owns the peer protocol; this skill owns the decision task below.
Reading it does not start a separate review or a second companion. Report an
unavailable dependency and pause only work requiring it.

Use the protocol's assessment and discussion stages to compare recommendations,
instead of defect findings. Replace its correction and integration stage with
the recommendation delivery below. Title the worker for this role and question,
such as “Decision peer — next iteration priority”, respecting an operator-given
name. This task requires no plan, repository, workflow binding or formal review
roster.

## Frame the decision

Establish the open question, desired outcome, constraints, settled decisions,
available evidence and who owns the decision from the request and accessible
sources. Distinguish an operator's tentative opinion from an accepted direction
or binding constraint. Preserve that standing in the brief; do not ask the
operator to settle the very choice the peers were asked to recommend.

Identify the criteria that would make one direction preferable. Use stated
priorities; label inferred priorities and assumptions. Ask through the caller's
designated channel only when a missing fact or value judgment prevents a useful
recommendation. When possible, give a conditional recommendation and name the
condition instead of guessing the operator's priorities.

Give the companion the question, relevant operator prompts and preferences,
criteria, evidence and limits, this skill's path, and the peer protocol's
required source, record and communication details. Include any proposal already
under consideration as a proposal, whoever authored it. Withhold the host's new
assessment until both initial positions are frozen; do not hide governing
context to manufacture independence.

## Develop and reconcile directions

Each peer examines the viable alternatives, including retaining the current
approach or deferring when relevant, and records a preferred direction. Explain
the decisive trade-off, supporting evidence, consequential assumptions and what
would change the recommendation. Investigate claims that could reverse the
choice within the task's authority. Distinguish evidence from inference and
preference; agreement cannot establish an unknown fact.

Exchange the frozen positions and use the peer protocol's equal discussion
procedure. Test the strongest objection to each preferred direction against the
decision criteria. Revise an option or combine compatible elements when that
better serves the objective; do not split the difference merely to agree.
Both peers must explicitly endorse the same recommendation, rationale and
material conditions before calling it joint agreement. Agreement may support a
bounded experiment or a conditional direction when evidence cannot yet support
a final choice; state what it would resolve and how its result would affect the
decision.

Use the protocol's limit of three completed paired rounds per disagreement,
preserving counts and positions across continuations. If disagreement remains,
return both recommendations, their shared premises and the decisive difference
to the operator. Identify the smallest missing evidence or operator judgment
that could resolve it. Do not report a host-selected answer as consensus.

## Deliver the recommendation

Lead with the agreed direction and its decisive reason. Include the main
trade-off, material assumptions or evidence gaps, and the next action the
operator can take. Preserve substantive dissent when agreement was not reached;
link retained discussion detail when needed rather than reproducing the debate.

Peer agreement is a recommendation, not operator acceptance or permission to
implement. This skill finishes with the recommendation and the peer protocol's
record retention and cleanup; it does not edit the proposal, record an accepted
decision or implement the choice. Return to the calling task, which may continue
under authority already granted without requesting it again. If the operator
has delegated the decision itself, state that authority and the resulting
decision accurately; implementation still follows the calling task's scope.

Preserve the question and source identities, frozen positions, discussion and
round counts, exact agreed recommendation or remaining disagreement, evidence
limits, launch settings and companion's final report in the selected records outside
tracked deliverables. Keep necessary support accessible for the result's useful
lifetime. Any subsequently requested durable decision record must state its
reason without relying on disposable debate records.
